- **"Your employee" card** under the rail fills in as answers land: the job (Bulk Calling, "In

## 2. Set them up (22:14–22:26)

**Question 1 of 2, "What job is this employee for?"** Three cards, each with a three-chip picture
of what happens:
- Instant Lead Calling: New lead → Calls in seconds → Qualifies them.
- Bulk Calling: Your list → Auto-dials each → Logs results.
- Inbound Calling: Someone calls → Answers & helps → Books or takes a message.
- Footer: "You can switch between these at any time." The founder reports that all three lead to
  the same next screen. Bulk Calling was the one taken forward.

**Question 2 of 2, "What language do they speak?"** Seven cards, each "Every call opens in X":
Telugu, Hindi, Tamil, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, English. After a primary is picked, an **"Also
speaks"** row appears: English (ticked and locked), plus one optional Indian language as a chip.
Rule shown: English is always on; add one more Indian language if your callers use it. The card
then reads "Telugu + English".

## 3. Describe the job (22:27–22:34)

- **Dark card, tagged FREE:** "What should your employee do on calls?", a few sentences in your own

  - The placeholder is a worked example (a dental clinic: call each enquiry, find the treatment,
    book a slot, note name and day).
  - There is a **microphone button** to speak the brief.
  - The button is "Build my employee".
- **"Or start from"** is a sideways-scrolling row of trade templates: Coaching institute, Hospital &
  clinic, Car dealership, Interiors & construction, Real estate, and more. Picking **Real estate**
  fills the box with a ready brief: work through past property enquiries, ask if still looking,
  area and budget now, whether anything changed, book a site visit this weekend, and note who
  already bought elsewhere so they are not chased again.
- **"Building your AI employee"** is a four-line checklist with a progress bar: Reading your brief →
  A few quick details → Planning the call, step by step → Choosing a voice. The right side narrates
  each one ("Reading your brief. A moment, then a few quick questions, if anything is missing").
- **"A few quick details", 1 to 3 questions, generated from the brief.** Each has Back, Skip, "Skip
  all and build" and "Edit brief", and a three-dash progress bar.
  1. "What is your business name?" — "So your test call sounds like your business."
  2. "What types of properties do you sell?" — tick boxes, generated for the trade: Residential,

  3. "What is your business location?" — "City or area"; the button becomes "Build my employee".
- "Building your employee": planning the call and choosing a voice, "takes a few seconds".

## 6. Arithmetic the screens support

- **Cost of the test call:** 2:00 at Premium ₹6.8/min = ₹13.60, and the record shows ₹13.60 and
  "13.6 credits used". So the onboarding test call **was charged at the voice's rate**, although tour
  step 3 calls a test call "free" and the setup says "Free to build".
- **Balance:** 36 credits left after the call means **about 50 credits at signup** (50 − 13.6 =
  36.4). That matches "50 free credits, no card" on their public pages (§7). "About 5 more calls"
  works out at about 7 credits, or about one minute of Premium, per call.
- **No phone check:** the phone number was entered on the launch screen. Only the own-number tick box
  stood in front of the call; no one-time code was seen in the screenshots.
- **Two phone numbers:** the number on the launch screen and the one on the calling screen differ
  (to be confirmed with the founder).

## 8. The founder's answers (23 Oct 2026)

- **Job choice.** Whether the test-call screen differs for Inbound or Instant was not checked; it
  may differ. Bulk was the path walked.
- **Brief.** The Real estate template was used unchanged, not spoken.
- **Quick details.** All five property types were ticked, and a location was typed (hence "Areas you
  cover: Hyderabad").
- **Name.** "Sanjana" was heard on the call, not a transcript artefact. No screen explained it.
- **Phone number.** The founder typed a different number after taking the launch-screen screenshot;
  Outpero called the number typed.
- **Voice.** No voice choice and no preview were offered during setup; P02 was picked for the
  employee.
- **How the call felt, founder's judgement.**
  - The phone rang about 15 seconds after launch.
  - Replies came well under 500–600 ms.
  - Talking over it was handled very well.
  - The Telugu, the emotion and the stress were "as if a real human would do based on
    conversational awareness".
  - The opening line was heard in full.
- **WhatsApp.** Nothing arrived after the call, which the founder expected: it was a test call
  simulating a real one.
- **Timing.** The 22:36 to 23:00 gap is unexplained and was judged unimportant.
- **Scope.** The compass, bell and the Overview, Actions, Outcomes and Settings tabs are deferred.

**The founder's reading of §4's observations.** The point of a trial test call is to show how the
agent really behaves in production. A confident "fixed" booking, a WhatsApp promise and a generic
next step are fine there, and could be better. Calevate takes this flow as the inspiration and
core, not as something to copy: the aim is a functionally better, research-backed version.
