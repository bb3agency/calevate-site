# Indian Telephony Regulatory & Contractual Research — Brief for Telecom Lawyer

**Prepared for:** Sole-proprietorship AI voice-agent SaaS (India), reselling programmable voice via a CPaaS (candidates: Vobiz, Plivo, Exotel)
**Research date:** September 17, 2026 (all sources read/verified on this date unless otherwise noted)
**Purpose:** This document assembles primary regulatory and contractual text — not legal opinion — to brief a telecom lawyer on two questions: (A) can the business sell/sub-allocate phone numbers to its clients, and (B) what is required, end-to-end, for the alternative model where the client owns the number and the business only provides the software layer.

**How to read this document:** Every factual claim is tagged by source type:
- **[REGULATOR/DoT]** / **[REGULATOR/TRAI]** — primary government/regulator text (Acts, Regulations, licences, circulars, enforcement notices)
- **[OPERATOR TERMS]** — Airtel/Jio/Vi/Tata Tele's own published contracts, FAQs, or DLT-platform pages
- **[PROVIDER TERMS]** — Vobiz/Plivo/Exotel's own published terms, docs, or KYC pages
- **[LAW FIRM COMMENTARY]** — secondary analysis by law firms; useful context, **not primary text**
- **[JOURNALISM]** — news reporting or company-registry aggregators; used only for corroboration, **not primary text**

Wherever a rule could not be located, this document says **NOT FOUND** rather than inferring an answer by analogy. Uncertainty is preserved, not smoothed over.

## PART A VERDICT — Can a sole proprietorship sell phone numbers to its clients?

**In one paragraph:** Based on the primary sources gathered, the honest answer is close to "no, not as a sole proprietorship, and not without becoming either a licensed reseller of a CPaaS or your own DoT-licensed entity" — but no primary DoT/TRAI text says this in so many words about SaaS/CPaaS resale specifically, so this verdict rests partly on an absence of a permission rather than an explicit prohibition. The concrete steps, each tied to the rule imposing it, are:

1. **Incorporate.** No primary source found (DoT Unified Licence, UL-VNO Licence, TRAI DLT operator FAQs, or any CPaaS's own compliance pages) contemplates a sole proprietorship as the licence-holder, VNO, or CPaaS "reseller" party. The UL-VNO licence text defines "Licensee" as "a registered Indian Company" ([DoT UL-VNO Licence Agreement](https://www.saras.gov.in/main/License%20Agreement/UL%20VNO.pdf), Annexure-I item 53, read 2026-09-17) with no mention of proprietorship anywhere. Every surveyed Indian competitor that lets customers get numbers in-product is incorporated as a Private Limited Company (or equivalent) — Exotel Techcom Pvt Ltd, MyOperator/VoiceTree Technologies Pvt Ltd, Ozonetel Systems Pvt Ltd, Knowlarity Communications Pvt Ltd, Cyllid Technologies Pvt Ltd (Skit.ai), GupShup Technology India Pvt Ltd — none was found operating this business model as a sole proprietorship (see A7 below and `a7_competitors.md`). Secondary commentary on DoT's Other Service Provider (OSP) registration route states the applicant "should be a Private Limited Company or a Limited Liability Partnership" and that "no one can follow the registration process... as an individual or a partnership" ([eStartIndia](https://www.estartindia.com/knowledge-hub/blog/what-is-a-dot-osp-license), [LAW FIRM COMMENTARY], read 2026-09-17) — though note OSP registration itself was abolished for most services in 2020 (see A1 below), this commentary is cited only to show that incorporation is treated as a structural gate across adjacent DoT registration regimes, not as binding OSP law today.
2. **Obtain your own DoT authorization (UL-VNO or similar), OR become a formally authorized reseller/partner of a licensed CPaaS/operator.** No CPaaS's public terms (Vobiz, Plivo, Exotel) offer a documented, self-serve "authorized reseller" programme for voice/number resale — Vobiz's own ToS bans resale "without prior written consent" and its "Partner with us" link routes to a generic sales-contact form with no published partner terms ([Vobiz ToS](https://www.vobiz.ai/terms/), read 2026-09-17); Plivo's ToS is silent on resale but its Additional Service Terms make **Plivo, not the customer**, the "customer of record" and the licence-carrying party ([Plivo Additional Service Terms](https://www.plivo.com/legal/additional-terms/) §3.2, read 2026-09-17); Exotel imposes an **absolute, no-exception ban on domestic resale** with no consent mechanism at all ([Exotel ToS](https://exotel.com/terms-of-service-exotel/) Clause 2, read 2026-09-17). This means the "authorised reseller" concept the user is hoping exists as a recognized middle path is, on the sources found, a commercial convention at best — not a status defined or granted in any licence text, and not a self-serve programme any CPaaS actually publishes.
3. **If reselling under someone else's licence, get that arrangement in writing and understand the licence text constrains it.** The Unified Licence itself prohibits sub-licensing/third-party interest "without prior written consent of Licensor" but explicitly permits a licensee to "appoint or employ franchisee, agents, distributors and employees" ([DoT Unified Licence](https://www.dot.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/05/af70ec29b07b112cdc5475d80afe8222.pdf) Chapter I Cond 6.1, read 2026-09-17). This is the only primary clause found that could textually support a franchisee/agent-style "authorised reseller" arrangement — but it is a clause governing the **licensee's own obligations to DoT**, not a right the user can invoke; whether a CPaaS's commercial relationship with the user would legally qualify as this kind of "agent/distributor" appointment is unresolved and is a lawyer question (see Lawyer Brief below).
4. **If numbers are provisioned via CPaaS accounts you control, understand you are not the subscriber of record and cannot make your clients such under current CPaaS terms.** Plivo: "Plivo owns the telephone number(s) assigned to you and reserves the right to refuse to port any telephone number(s) at its discretion" ([Plivo Additional Service Terms](https://www.plivo.com/legal/additional-terms/) §1.1-1.2). Exotel: "Exotel is not creating any right or interest in the Exophone Numbers in favour of the Customer... except for the limited purpose of accessing and using the Product" ([Exotel ToS](https://exotel.com/terms-of-service-exotel/) Clause 2). Neither CPaaS's terms contemplate the user's own clients becoming a recognized sub-subscriber; the user's account remains the sole contractual point of contact and liability holder.
5. **Expect UCC/TCCCPR liability to attach at the account/Sender level, not per-client-number, which is a direct business risk of housing multiple clients' traffic in one account.** TCCCPR's penalty regime disconnects "all telecom resources of the sender" on repeated violation and blacklists that Sender for up to two years ([TCCCPR 2018](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-09/RegulationUcc19072018.pdf) Reg 25(6), read 2026-09-17) — if the CPaaS account (not the individual client) is treated as the Sender, one client's violation could jeopardize numbers belonging to all other clients on that account. This is NOT explicitly resolved either way in the text (see B6 below) but is a live structural risk under the current one-account-many-clients model.

**If the honest answer is "incorporate and obtain reseller authorisation" — that is being said plainly here**, with the caveat that "reseller authorisation" itself was not found defined or obtainable as a formal status in any primary text; it may in practice mean privately negotiating better contractual terms with an incorporated entity, not obtaining a licence.

## PART B — Operational Runbook: Client-Owned Number, End to End

**Scenario:** A client wants an AI voice agent. The client will own the number; the user's software will place/receive calls. Numbered steps below; every step marks who acts, where, what's needed, and cost/time where known. **Steps where the authoritative answer was not found are marked clearly.**

### Step 1 — Client chooses a number source and buys the number
**Who acts:** The client (the small business), not the user.
**Two routes exist**, compared in detail in `b1_number_purchase.md`:

- **Route 1a — via a CPaaS (Exotel/Plivo/Vobiz), in the client's own name.**
  - Exotel: separate KYC document tables by entity type. For a **sole proprietorship**: "Certificate of Incorporation: GST Registration certificate/MSME Registration certificate/Incorporation certificate · Shop establishment certificate (trade license) · FSSAI registration · Import export license · Form C" + PAN + address proof + photo ([Exotel Support](https://support.exotel.com/support/solutions/articles/35760-where-do-i-upload-my-kyc-verification-docs-and-what-documents-qualify-for-kyc-), read 2026-09-17). KYC review: 1–3 business days (up to 5 if manual review) ([Exotel Docs](https://developer.exotel.com/docs/getting-started/kyc-verification)). Exact ₹ rental price: **NOT FOUND** on Exotel's public pages.
  - Plivo: single document requirement regardless of entity type — "one valid business document: GST Certificate, COI, or Udyam Registration" ([Plivo Docs](https://plivo.com/docs/numbers/rent-india-numbers)). Review ~5 minutes once submitted. Cost: **₹200.00/month** for domestic number rental ([Plivo India Pricing](https://www.plivo.com/phone-numbers/pricing/in/)).
  - Vobiz: two-track KYC — "Individual" (Aadhaar + personal PAN, explicitly for "individual business owners") vs "Company" (business PAN + GST) ([Vobiz KYC Docs](https://www.vobiz.ai/docs/compliance/india/kyc)). Approval ~2 minutes, automated. Usage pricing ₹0.65/min inbound and outbound; exact monthly rental fee **NOT FOUND**.
  - **Critical limitation for this route:** none of the three CPaaS's own terms make the client the true "subscriber of record" in a portable sense — Plivo and Exotel both explicitly retain ownership/customer-of-record status over CPaaS-issued numbers. A number bought this way is not equivalent to a carrier-issued number the client can freely redirect elsewhere; it exists inside that CPaaS's own account and is licensed for use, not owned, by the client.

- **Route 1b — via a licensed operator (Airtel/Jio/Vi/Tata Tele), in the client's own name.**
  - This route is **sales-assisted, not self-serve**, for all four operators — no public document checklist splitting sole-prop vs pvt ltd was found for any of them.
  - Jio publishes the clearest self-serve pricing found across all four operators: **₹999 refundable security deposit + ₹499/month rental** for a toll-free number ([Jio Business Toll-Free](https://www.jio.com/business/services/voice-and-collaboration/toll-free-number/)). KYC documents: **NOT FOUND** published.
  - Airtel's only public document checklist is a **2020-dated PDF** (still live) showing a ₹5,900 registration fee (stated waived as of Feb 2020) and a ₹50,000 refundable security deposit for **bulk business connections** (not confirmed current, not confirmed applicable to a single virtual number) ([Airtel Document Checklist](https://www.airtel.in/business/commercial-communication/assets/documents/Document%20Checklist.pdf)).
  - Vi Business and Tata Tele Smartflo: **NOT FOUND** — both route only to a sales callback form, with no published KYC list, pricing, deposit, or timeline.
  - DoT's own "Additional KYC Instructions in Respect of Business Connections" require, from the customer, CIN (where applicable), business licence/trade registration, GST certificate (if applicable), and an end-user list — but per legal-analysis commentary, "does not specify a separate KYC-document requirement or procedural distinction between a sole proprietorship and a company" ([law-firm summary of DoT circular](https://www.dmd.law/publications/additional-kyc-instructions-in-respect-of-business-connections/), [LAW FIRM COMMENTARY], read 2026-09-17). The same commentary notes business connections are capped at 100 per customer, validity may not exceed one year, and **resale is prohibited** by the underlying DoT rule for these connections.

**Cost/timeline summary for Step 1:** CPaaS route is faster (minutes to ~5 business days) and has transparent, if incomplete, self-serve pricing; operator route is slower (activation "24 hours to a few business days" per Airtel's own blog, once a sales conversation has concluded) with far less published pricing transparency.

### Step 2 — Client registers as a DLT Principal Entity (PE) — REQUIRED FOR PROMOTIONAL/COMMERCIAL COMMUNICATION, see Step 2a for the threshold question
**Who acts:** The client. TRAI's own text is explicit that "all PEs have to initiate all the related registration request by themselves" — a telemarketer/delivery partner (including a CPaaS or the user's platform) **cannot register on the client's behalf** ([Tata Tele DLT FAQ](https://www.tatatelebusiness.com/pdf/dlt/FAQ.pdf), read 2026-09-17).

**Portal choice:** Any one Access Provider's DLT platform (Airtel/Vi-VILPOWER/Tata/Jio/BSNL) is sufficient — a Registered Telemarketer (RTM) ID is stated to be "unique across all access providers/DLT platforms" once issued ([Tata Tele DLT FAQ](https://www.tatatelebusiness.com/pdf/dlt/FAQ.pdf)). The underlying registration platforms across operators use a shared/near-identical template (Airtel's and Vi/VILPOWER's PE classification lists and LoA definitions are textually verbatim identical), so portal choice is largely a matter of account-management convenience, not a legal distinction — **directly relevant to the user's current lean toward VILPOWER: fee-wise there is no advantage, since VILPOWER's TM-DF fee (₹50,000) matches Airtel's and Tata's exactly (see Step 2b).**

**Documents/procedure:** Business PAN, complete address, authorized-person mobile/email, PAN + address proof uploads ([Airtel DLT FAQ](https://dltconnect.airtel.in/faq/); [Tata Tele DLT FAQ](https://www.tatatelebusiness.com/pdf/dlt/FAQ.pdf)). "Sole Proprietor" is an explicitly recognized PE classification category at both Airtel and VILPOWER ("i. Individual, ii. Sole Proprietor, iii. Partnership Firm, iv. Private/Public/Govt. Limited Company, v. Limited Liability Partnership, vi. Trust, Association, Security & Club (TASC), vii. Govt. Entity") — **but no operator publishes a document checklist specific to the Sole Proprietor category**; it falls under the same generic "PAN + address proof" bucket as every other non-Govt/SEBI entity type at Tata ("Others, Private or Public Limited - PAN & Address Proof (GST certificate, COAI...etc.)").

### Step 2a — Is DLT/PE registration even required for this client's use case? (see PART B, Section B3 discussion below — this is a threshold question, not a formality)
Before the client spends money/time on PE registration, this should be checked: TCCCPR only mandates registration for "Commercial Communication," and explicitly carves "Transactional" and "Service" calls/messages out of the UCC definition ([TCCCPR 2018](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-09/RegulationUcc19072018.pdf) Reg 2(bw)). If the client is only calling existing, consenting customers for service/transactional purposes (not cold promotional outreach), the case for needing PE/TM registration at all is weaker — but this is **not fully resolved by the text found** (see B3 below); treat as a genuine open question for the lawyer, not a settled exemption.

### Step 3 — Client registers/links a Telemarketer (TM) — only if the client (or the user, on the client's behalf) will be sending registered promotional communications
The Telemarketer categories, per Tata's FAQ, are:
- **TM-DF (Delivery Function):** "has direct connectivity with telecom service provider(s)" and delivers SMS/voice.
- **TM-AF (Aggregator Function):** aggregates traffic from multiple enterprises without direct telecom connectivity.
- **RTM (Registered Telemarketer):** any TM registered with at least one Access Provider/DLT platform.

A Letter of Authorisation (LoA) is the mandatory instrument for a PE to delegate DLT functions to a TM: "Letter of Authorisation (LoA) shall be signed by authorised signatory of a principal entity to allow/delegate select functions to a telemarketer on behalf of the entity" ([VILPOWER FAQ](https://www.vilpower.in/faq/); text is verbatim identical at Airtel). Required LoA fields: name of PE, signatory + designation + contact, business details/purpose, name of TM + Key Account Manager. **This LoA is specific to the DLT PE→TM delegation relationship — it is a different instrument from a carrier-level "authorization to use my number via your infrastructure" LoA, which is discussed in Step 4 and remains largely unaddressed by primary text.**

### Step 4 — THE PART THE USER MOST NEEDS: getting calls to/from the client's number through the user's software
This is the central unresolved question of the entire research. Four technically recognized mechanisms exist in principle; each is described below along with what was and was not found regarding whether Indian carrier/CPaaS contracts actually permit it.

**(a) SIP trunk delegation** — the client's number is connected via a SIP trunk to the user's platform/CPaaS so calls route through the user's software while nominally remaining "on" the client's number.
- **NOT FOUND:** Any Indian carrier's or CPaaS's terms explicitly describing or permitting this exact arrangement (a client's operator-issued number, SIP-trunked to third-party cloud infrastructure not owned by the operator).
- **Closest primary text found — Airtel's Telecom Consumers Charter, EPBAX clause:** "Unless specifically allowed by Airtel, the Customer shall not change the configuration of its EPBAX set... resulting in only the Pilot number being transmitted irrespective of the extension... In case the Customer wishes to use the EPBAX with private network & PSTN, they must have proper authorisation from DoT/TRAI and must ensure that the separation of traffic is done through logical partitioning" ([Airtel Telecom Consumers Charter 2025](https://assets.airtel.in/static-assets/cms/Airtel_Telecom_Consumers_Charter_English_2025.pdf), read 2026-09-17). This clause (i) confirms Airtel does restrict CLI/pilot-number transmission configuration changes but (ii) implies a permission process exists ("unless specifically allowed by Airtel") without describing what that process is, and (iii) is written for on-premises EPBAX/PBX hardware, not modern cloud SIP-trunk/API-based call origination — it is unclear whether it extends to the user's scenario at all.
- **Plivo's Additional Service Terms §3.6** prohibits "toll bypass, unauthorized routing of traffic, or any other means of circumventing the licensed carriage, interconnection, or settlement arrangements of underlying telecommunications operators" — a SIP-delegation arrangement not properly authorized by the underlying carrier could implicate this clause, but the clause does not define what counts as "authorized" in this context.

**(b) Call forwarding** — the client sets up simple carrier-level call forwarding from their number to a number the user's platform monitors.
- **NOT FOUND:** any Indian operator's consumer/business terms addressing call forwarding to third-party software/infrastructure specifically (as opposed to forwarding to another personal number).

**(c) Number hosting** — the client's number is "hosted" by the CPaaS/user's platform under some formal hosting agreement short of full porting.
- **NOT FOUND:** any primary Indian source describing a "hosted number" status distinct from either full ownership-by-the-CPaaS (as with Plivo/Exotel-issued numbers) or full portability under MNP.

**(d) Full porting (MNP)** — the client ports their number away from the original carrier into the CPaaS's own numbering pool.
- **This IS a real, well-documented mechanism** ([TRAI MNP Regulations 2009, consolidated](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-10/RegulationsMNPEng13122018.pdf), read 2026-09-17): porting is subscriber-initiated via SMS to a short code, with a Letter of Authorisation required specifically **for corporate mobile numbers** (Reg 8(6)), covering up to 100 numbers per LOA (raised from 50 per the 2018 Explanatory Memorandum). Timelines: 2 working days intra-LSA, 4 working days inter-LSA porting, per Airtel's Telecom Consumers Charter.
- **Critical gap, confirmed directly from the primary MNP regulation text itself, not just "not yet found":** "The page contains no provision concerning: call forwarding; SIP trunking; third-party call origination... or use of a ported or non-ported number as a delegated, forwarded, or third-party calling identity." **The porting-LOA is an instrument for the act of changing operators — it is not a general delegation/hosting instrument**, despite surface similarity to international CPaaS LOA templates (Twilio, Bandwidth) and to the DLT PE→TM LoA. If the client fully ports their number into the CPaaS's own pool, the CPaaS then becomes the customer/subscriber of record for that number (per Plivo/Exotel's own terms) — meaning the number is no longer really "client-owned" in the way the user's brief envisions; it becomes CPaaS-owned, with the client relegated to bare usage rights, same as if the client had bought the number directly from that CPaaS in Step 1a.

**For OUTBOUND caller-ID presentation specifically:** Plivo's own materials (not independently re-verified from a primary Plivo statement in this pass but consistent with the user's own prior research) reportedly say verified caller ID is unavailable in India; Exotel says masking an owned number is not permitted. **No lawful mechanism for presenting a client's own validly-assigned number as caller ID from third-party infrastructure was found in any primary source** — not in the MNP Regulations, not in Airtel's Consumer Charter (beyond the ambiguous EPBAX clause), not in Plivo's or Exotel's terms, and not in the DoT identifier-tampering advisory (see Step 5). This is the single largest confirmed gap in the entire research.

### Step 5 — Legal risk overlay on Step 4: is authorized delegation "identifier tampering"?
DoT's Telecommunications Act 2023, Section 42(3)(c) criminalizes "unauthorised tampering with telecommunication identifiers such as Calling Line Identification (CLI), IP addresses, or IMEI numbers"; Section 42(3)(e) criminalizes fraudulently obtaining an identifier; Section 42(6) extends liability to abetment; offences are cognizable and non-bailable (read via [LAW FIRM COMMENTARY - LexOrbis](https://www.lexorbis.com/dot-advisory-on-telecom-identifier-tampering-a-strong-regulatory-push-to-safeguard-communication-networks/) and [LawChakra](https://lawchakra.in/legal-updates/caller-id-spoofing-ban-dot-illegal/), summarizing a DoT advisory dated 18 February 2025 — the advisory's own PDF was not directly located).

**NOT FOUND:** any text — regulator, commentary, or otherwise — that expressly addresses whether *authorized* display of a validly-assigned third-party number (as opposed to spoofing an arbitrary or fake number) constitutes "unauthorised tampering" under this section. Every quoted example concerns spoofing/faking a number, not authorized delegation of a real one. This is the single most important open legal question flagged for the lawyer (see Lawyer Brief below).

Separately, TRAI has approved in principle (but not yet implemented, per commentary dated ~November 2025) a Calling Name Presentation (CNAP) verified-caller-ID service relying strictly on KYC-verified operator data — signaling the regulatory direction is toward tighter identity-to-number binding, which may make any delegation arrangement harder to defend over time, not easier ([SCC Times commentary](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/11/01/trai-approves-verified-caller-id-cnap-scctimes-update/), [LAW FIRM COMMENTARY]).

### Step 6 — Who is the Telemarketer in this arrangement?
TCCCPR defines "Sender" as whoever owns the number/header, asserts the CLI, causes the communication, authorizes it, or benefits from the promoted goods/services (Reg 2(bf)) — under a plain reading, **the client is the Sender** (they own the number and are the beneficiary). "Telemarketer" is defined functionally as anyone "engaged in the activity of transmission or delivery of commercial communication or scrubbing or aggregation" (Reg 2(bp)), with a specific sub-category **TM-VCF ("Telemarketer for voice calling function with Telecom Resource Connectivity for voice calls to Access Provider")** whose duties include "using virtual identities to make voice calls on a secure IP-based VPN with OAP and not disclosing real identities to any other entities than authorized."

**NOT SETTLED by the text alone:** whether a SaaS that only runs the software (without itself being RTM-registered, without owning the number, without owning the client list) is thereby pulled into the TM-VCF registration requirement merely by virtue of technically carrying/routing the call. The regulation defines the functional role broadly enough that "engaged in transmission or delivery" plausibly captures the user's platform, but no text was found applying this definition to a software-only intermediary as distinct from a traditional aggregator/carrier-connectivity business. **This is the second core lawyer question** (see below).

### Step 7 — The shared-account question: does housing ten clients' numbers/traffic in one CPaaS account change the legal picture?
- Provider terms found do **not explicitly permit or prohibit** one account carrying multiple end-businesses' traffic as such — Vobiz's terms state the account holder "remain[s] responsible for all charges incurred under your account, including API usage by authorized or unauthorized third parties," and Vobiz's own sub-account documentation requires each `customer_use` sub-account to complete its own KYC before it can place calls (`kyc_calls_blocked: true` until verified) — suggesting Vobiz's own architecture anticipates multi-tenant use and gates it on a per-sub-account KYC basis, which is the closest thing found to explicit provider accommodation of the user's model.
- **DLT registration is per-legal-entity, not per-account:** Tata's FAQ confirms "every legal Entity needs to be registered separately on the platform as different Principal Entity" — this is a structural argument that DLT compliance tracks the client's own PE registration, not the CPaaS account, which would argue AGAINST one client's DLT violation directly harming another client's DLT status (their PE registrations are legally distinct even if hosted in the same CPaaS account).
- **However, TCCCPR's enforcement text disconnects "all telecom resources of the sender"** (Reg 25(6)) — if "the sender" is interpreted at the account level (the CPaaS account, which is legally the user's) rather than at the level of each individual client-PE, then one client's violation absolutely could jeopardize numbers belonging to unrelated clients sharing that account. **This tension between the DLT per-entity registration structure and TCCCPR's per-Sender enforcement mechanism is not resolved by any text found and is the third lawyer question.**

## PART 4 — The Lawyer Brief: Questions Public Sources Cannot Settle

These are phrased for direct answer by a telecom lawyer, because no primary text resolved them:

1. **Can a SaaS act as an authorised reseller under a UL-VNO without its own licence?** No licence text defines an "authorised reseller" status for a party that neither holds a UL-VNO nor is itself the CPaaS's own corporate structure. The Unified Licence permits a licensee to appoint "franchisee, agents, distributors" (Cond 6.1) — does a SaaS reselling CPaaS-provisioned numbers to its own clients qualify as this kind of agent/distributor of the CPaaS's underlying licence, or does it require its own authorization regardless of any commercial agreement with the CPaaS?

2. **Who is the telemarketer when the number belongs to the client and the platform belongs to the vendor?** TCCCPR defines "Sender" (owner/beneficiary — plausibly the client) and "Telemarketer"/TM-VCF (whoever transmits/delivers/carries — plausibly the SaaS) as distinct roles, but no text applies this framework to a pure software intermediary that owns neither the number nor the customer relationship. Does running the technical delivery layer, without RTM registration, expose the SaaS to unregistered-telemarketer liability regardless of the client's own PE/TM status?

3. **Does authorized delegation of a validly-assigned number constitute "unauthorised tampering" under Telecommunications Act 2023 Section 42(3)(c)?** Every primary/commentary source found on identifier tampering addresses spoofing or faking a number, not authorized display of a real, validly-assigned third-party number under a delegation/hosting arrangement. Is there a lawful mechanism (SIP trunk delegation, hosting agreement, authorization letter) that puts such an arrangement outside Section 42's scope, and if so, what paperwork would evidence "authorization" to a regulator or in a dispute?

4. **Does a client's carrier or CPaaS contract permit the client to direct their number's traffic to third-party infrastructure at all?** Airtel's Consumers Charter restricts EPBAX/CLI configuration changes "unless specifically allowed by Airtel" but does not describe that permission process, and is written for on-premises hardware, not cloud SIP trunking. Plivo's terms prohibit "unauthorized routing of traffic" circumventing "licensed carriage, interconnection, or settlement arrangements" without defining what routing would be "authorized." Is there a standard operator/CPaaS permission process for this, and if none exists today, is the arrangement contractually breach-prone regardless of DoT/TRAI law?

5. **Is TCCCPR's "disconnect all telecom resources of the sender" enforcement mechanism applied per legal entity (the client-PE) or per account (the CPaaS account housing several clients)?** DLT PE registration is confirmed to be per-legal-entity, but TCCCPR's penalty text disconnects "the sender's" resources without clarifying whether "the sender" means the CPaaS account holder or each individual client. This materially affects whether pooling multiple clients' numbers in one CPaaS account creates cross-client contagion risk on enforcement.

6. **Does the transactional/service call exemption from UCC actually exempt a caller from PE/TM/DLT registration itself, or only from UCC enforcement consequences?** TCCCPR's Reg 2(bw) excludes transactional and service calls from the *definition* of UCC, but the registration mandate in Reg 3/10/32 is framed around "Commercial Communication" generally, and the text read does not show transactional/service calls being carved out of the underlying Commercial Communication/registration requirement itself, only out of UCC. If the user's clients call only existing consenting customers for service/transactional purposes, is PE/TM registration required at all, or is it optional risk-mitigation?

7. **Does incorporation alone resolve A1-A6, or does the user also need a UL-VNO/CPaaS reseller agreement even as a Private Limited Company?** Every competitor surveyed is incorporated, but incorporation and telecom-licensing/reseller-authorization are conceptually separate requirements per the sources found — incorporation appears to be a necessary but not sufficient condition based on the UL-VNO's own minimum capital requirements (₹1 crore for standard Access Service, or a lighter Category B path with CA-certified net worth for non-company entities, which is itself ambiguous as to eligibility).

8. **What specifically must a Sole Proprietorship PE submit that a Private Limited PE does not, and vice versa?** Every operator's public PE classification list recognizes "Sole Proprietor" as a category, but no operator publishes the entity-specific document checklist — this can likely only be resolved by actually starting the registration on a live portal, which a lawyer or the user should do with legal review of what gets submitted.

Enforcement statistics exist in aggregate (731,120 UTM notices in 2025) but no CPaaS-specific case was located despite direct searching.

## A Note on the VILPOWER Question

Since the user's most recent steering signal was a lean toward VILPOWER (Vi) for DLT registration: the research found **no cost advantage** to this choice — Vi's TM-DF security deposit (₹50,000) and PE/TM registration fees (₹5,000 each) are identical to Airtel's and Tata's. The one operational difference confirmed is PE registration validity (Vi: 1 year, renewable with a 30-day grace period; Tata: 5 years; Airtel: not confirmed for PE specifically, only TM at 5 years) — a shorter validity period means more frequent renewal administration for VILPOWER, which is a mild point against it, not for it, if minimizing renewal overhead matters. Since the underlying registration platforms are templated near-identically across operators, the practical decision should rest on portal usability, support responsiveness, and possibly the specific CPaaS/operator relationship already in place — not on registration fees, which are the same everywhere researched.
