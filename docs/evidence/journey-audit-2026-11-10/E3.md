## Fixed

### E3-01 (P2, wrong data on screen and in a data-access export): a claim the dispatcher refused kept the try it stamped

- **Where:**
  - `apps/workers/campaign_dispatch.py`, the claim in `_dispatch_for_campaign` and
    `_refuse_contact`.
  - The claim sets `attempts + 1` and `last_attempt_at = now()`.
  - `dispatch_call`'s `on_reserved` link sets `last_call_id`.
  - On a refusal that rang nobody, `_refuse_contact` refunded the attempt for transient
    rules only. It never restored `last_attempt_at` or `last_call_id`. For a person-level
    refusal it kept the attempt too.
- **What the person and the owner saw:**
  - **The campaign screen.** `ContactResults.PersonOutcome` shows "Not called yet · Last
    22:30" for a person nobody rang. One who was rung once and then held by the halt shows
    the refusal's time instead of their real call. The list sorts by that time.
  - **The data-access export.** `compliance/export.py` gives the subject `attempts` and
    `last_attempt_at`. It tells someone on the do-not-call list "attempts: 1" with a time,
    although no call was made. With E3-02 below, that time could be 23:15 IST, which reads
    as a call attempt outside TRAI hours.
  - **The "people called" figure.** A dial the voice platform refused after the line was
    reserved (a 429 for full lines, a 402, its own opt-out list) left `last_call_id` on a
    call that never rang. `results.summary` counts `tried` as `last_call_id IS NOT NULL`,
    so CampaignResults' "N of M people called" counted that person.
- **Fix:**
  - The claim's `picked` CTE carries each contact's `prior_at` and `prior_call`.
  - `_refuse_contact` now takes `prior: PriorTry` and writes both back.
  - A person-level refusal before the dial settles `dnc_blocked` with the attempt handed
    back (`_SETTLE_PERSON_REFUSED_UNDIALLED`).
  - The recall path (`settle_recalled_contact`) is unchanged on purpose: its dial had
    already left for the voice platform.
- **Tests (each fails on the pre-fix source):**
  - `test_a_person_refused_for_do_not_call_before_the_dial_carries_no_try`
  - `test_a_refusal_by_the_hour_puts_the_previous_try_back`
  - `test_a_dial_refused_after_its_line_was_reserved_is_not_a_person_called`

### E3-02 (P2, wasted work and false records all night): outside 09:00-21:00 IST a campaign with no window of its own was claimed and refunded every tick

- **Where:** `_plan_tenant` skipped a campaign whose OWN narrowed window was closed. It
  asked nothing about the platform window: `campaign_window_open(None, …)` is True by
  design.
- **Failure:**
  - From 21:00 to 09:00, every 30 seconds, the tick claimed `slots` contacts of every
    running campaign with no window of its own. `check_dispatch` refused each one
    `calling_hours`, and each was refunded with a 30-minute wait.
  - A 1,000-person list was cycled through overnight. Every person got a night-time
    `last_attempt_at` (E3-01).
  - Each refusal raised the `compliance_blocks{rule="calling_hours"}` metric, thousands of
    times a night. That buries the real blocks the campaign-stall runbook sends operators
    to.
  - In the morning everyone was spread over the next 30 minutes instead of being due at
    09:00.
- **Fix:**
  - New `campaigns.service.campaign_hours_open(calling_hours, now_ist)`: the platform
    window AND the campaign's own.
  - `_plan_tenant` uses it, so no one is claimed outside hours.
  - `campaign_progress`'s "waiting for calling hours" uses the same helper (it had this
    check inline), so the screen and the dispatcher share one definition.
  - The per-dial gate still enforces the platform window on every dial.
- **Test:** `test_a_tick_outside_the_platform_hours_leaves_the_list_untouched`. It also
  asserts that no compliance block is recorded.
- `tests/campaigns_test.py::test_outside_calling_hours_the_contact_waits_instead_of_burning_an_attempt`
  asserted the old claim-and-refund side effect (`next_attempt_at` set). It now asserts the
  contact is untouched; the attempt is still never spent.

### E3-03 (P2, stuck state): a campaign outside its calling hours was never visited, so it stayed "running" with nobody left

- **Where:** `_plan_tenant` used `continue` past a closed window. `_dispatch_for_campaign`
  holds the reaper, the unanswered-dial settle and `_settle_finished_campaign`, so none of
  them ran.
- **Failure:**
  - A campaign narrowed to 09:00-12:00 dials its last person at 11:58. The call ends at
    12:03 and the pipeline marks the contact connected.
  - The campaign then says "running" until 09:00 the next day. `campaign.completed`, which
    a client can send to an endpoint or a sheet, also waits until then.
  - The same applies to busy or no-answer dials placed just before the window closed: they
    stayed "calling" overnight.
  - With E3-02 the platform window now closes the plan too, so without this fix every
    campaign would have had this defect from 21:00.
- **Fix:**
  - A campaign outside its hours goes on a `settling` list. `_dispatch_fleet` visits it
    every tick, whatever the line pool holds.
  - The visit is `_settle_out_of_hours`: `_reap_stuck_dialing` (with the unanswered
    settle) and then `_settle_finished_campaign`. It dials nobody.
  - Per-tenant failures are isolated like the other steps (step `settle`).
  - A tenant whose line budget is spent no longer stops the window check for its later
    campaigns: `break` became `continue`.
- **Test:** `test_a_campaign_whose_last_call_ends_after_its_window_completes` (fails
  pre-fix: `'running' == 'completed'`).

## Checked, no defect found

Every journey step was followed at source:

- **Create.** Narrowing-only window, HH:MM, half-open.
- **Lists.** Upload, CRM leads and lens go through `add_contacts` with dedupe, India-only
  and the D2-02 lock.
- **Launch.** Scrub plus gate.
- **The per-tick money pause.** Fires before the claim.
- **Standing gate and complaint spike.** Both checked before the claim.
- **Per-contact checks.** Status, window and halt are re-read past the cache, then the
  gate.
- **Unconfirmed dials.** Terminal, never re-rung.
- **Full lines.** One-minute wait.
- **Retry ladder.** On the database clock.
- **Unanswered settle.** 2 minutes for busy/no answer, 20 for failed.
- **Reaper.** Outlives the longest permitted call.
- **Completion.** CAS from `running`, with re-arm for repeats.
- **Stop.** Calls off call-backs.
- **"Call them again".** Only from a finished or stopped source, and only unreached
  statuses.
- **Archive.** Never dialled.
- **Credit.** The resume after a top-up is a CAS on the `out_of_credit` reason.
- **Resume.** `assert_resumable` refuses while the money or complaint reason still holds.
- **Counts.** `contacts_page` counts and `summary` outcomes share `_OUTCOME_SQL`, and
  the list's `connected` agrees with it (the `completed` contact status is never written).

## NOT FIXED

- **P3:** a campaign the client pauses mid-batch, then resumes at once, finds the contacts
  that batch had claimed waiting 30 minutes. `campaign_not_running` is refunded on
  `_SETTLE_REFUNDED`. The person is not harmed; this is pacing only. Changing the wait is
  a product choice.
- **P3:** while the shared pool is saturated, or the engine's 429 hold-off is running,
  `_dispatch_fleet` returns `pool_saturated` before any in-hours campaign is visited. A
  campaign that finished in that window completes when a line frees. This is transient,
  and the tick's starvation alarm covers it.
- **P3:** `results.summary.calls` counts not-placed call rows (`not_placed_reason`
  set). The screen does not show that figure. `trials.py` already excludes those rows for
  its own count.
- **UNVERIFIED (narrow race):** suppose the dispatcher reads `credits_exhausted` as true,
  a top-up then commits, and its `apply_inbound_credit_state` job runs before the
  dispatcher's pause commits. The campaign then stays paused `out_of_credit` with credit
  in the wallet. A way out exists: Resume is allowed, because `assert_resumable` re-reads
  the wallet. But the "resumes by itself" promise would fail. Not reproduced.
