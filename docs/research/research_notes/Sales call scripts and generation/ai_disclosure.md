# AI disclosure at the start of a call: placement, wording, and its effect on trust, hang-ups and conversion (India, Oct 2026)

All sources read 30 Oct 2026 unless stated. Evidence classes:
**PEER-REVIEWED** (journal text read), **PEER-REVIEWED-ABSTRACT** (only the abstract or a listing was read),
**WORKING-PAPER**, **PRIMARY-LEGAL** (the statute or regulation text was read), **SECONDARY-LEGAL** (law-firm or news
summary), **VENDOR** (a company with a stake in the outcome), **SURVEY**, **REPORTED** (repo-internal or relayed, not
re-read here), **UNKNOWN**.

Where it applies, scope is this: Calevate's outbound agents volunteer the AI disclosure by default. The per-agent switch remains, and a truthful answer when asked is enforced server-side.

## 1. Experimental and field evidence: what disclosure does to hang-ups, trust and purchase

### Takeaway
The best randomized pre-LLM evidence found that disclosing before the pitch cuts purchases sharply and that most of the loss comes from immediate hang-ups. The newer evidence, from LLM-era agents and the 2026 Management Science paper, finds a much smaller penalty or none at all. It also finds that human-like delivery and capable handling offset the penalty, and that callers often detect AI anyway. Observational inbound data even associates disclosure with fewer hang-ups. The penalty is real but closes over time, and how the call is handled after the disclosure matters more than whether disclosure happens.

### Cited Findings
- **Luo, Tong, Fang & Qu (2019), Marketing Science 38(6):937-947 (PEER-REVIEWED, full text read).** This was a field experiment at a Chinese fintech lender. 6,255 customers were randomized to receive outbound loan-renewal sales calls under six conditions. The disclosure was a single sentence ("Dear customer, I am the AI voice chatbot of the company XYZ"); the control greeting was "I am the service agent of the company XYZ". [PDF, Temple Fox](https://www.fox.temple.edu/sites/fox/files/Frontiers-Machines-versus-Humans-The-Impact-of-Artificial-Intelligence-Chatbot-Disclosure-on-Customer-Purchases.pdf)
  - Purchase rates were 0.237 for the undisclosed bot, 0.251 for proficient humans and 0.049 for inexperienced humans. With **disclosure before the conversation the rate was 0.048, down 79.7%**. Disclosure after the conversation but before the decision gave **0.110**, and disclosure after the decision gave 0.232. (Table 3, same PDF)
  - Hang-ups, defined as the customer ending the call within 5 s of the disclosure, were **56.3%** with disclosure before the conversation, 4.5% after the conversation and 0% in the other four conditions. Mean call length was 10.3 s with disclosure before the conversation, against about 64 s with no disclosure. (Table 3, same PDF)
  - Mechanism, from post-call surveys and bootstrapped mediation: people who knew they were talking to a bot rated it less knowledgeable and less empathetic. Voice-mining showed the bot was objectively as competent as proficient humans, so the authors read the penalty as subjective bias rather than deception. They found no complaints or cancellations. **Customers with prior AI experience showed a significantly smaller penalty** (positive interaction term, p<0.01). (same PDF, pp. 944-945)
  - Caveats: 2019 pre-LLM technology, China, a single loan-renewal offer to existing borrowers, a 77% male sample, and calls placed during work hours.
- **Xu, Dai & Yan, "Identity Disclosure and Anthropomorphism in Voice Chatbot Design: A Field Experiment", Management Science 72(1):223-241, 2026 (PEER-REVIEWED-ABSTRACT; full text not read).** 11,000 truck drivers were randomized to outbound calls from a voice-bot dispatcher on a Chinese freight platform. Per the abstract as surfaced in search results, **disclosure reduced response rates by about 11%**, and anthropomorphic features (interjections, fillers) raised response, conversation length and order acceptance by about 5.6-26.9%, offsetting the disclosure penalty through trust. [INFORMS DOI page](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/epdf/10.1287/mnsc.2022.03833); [SUFE listing](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/395943). The numbers come from the search-result summary of the abstract: INFORMS and EBSCO returned 403 here.
- **Habel, Zawadzki & Freise, "Selling with AI Voice Agents: Does Disclosure Still Matter?", SSRN 7013259 (WORKING-PAPER; abstract seen only as a search snippet, full text 403).** A randomized field experiment (N=1,337 customers reached) reports that **disclosing an LLM-era AI voice agent no longer reduced conversion compared with not disclosing**. A second field study (N=366) found AI agents still sold well below humans, attributed to weak handling of the sales process. An online study (N=238) found people recognize AI agents whether or not they are told, and are less willing to keep talking when they expect less empathy or sincerity. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/7013259.pdf?abstractid=7013259&mirid=1). Industry, country and publication status are UNKNOWN.
- **Upfirst, "What makes people hang up on AI receptionists?" (VENDOR, observational).** 450,702 **inbound** calls across 503 AI receptionists were analysed with logistic regression on two hang-up definitions (<15 s; caller never spoke). **Disclosing AI was associated with about 20% fewer hang-ups**, and a recording notice with about 30% fewer (the author calls this unproven causally). Opening with the owner being unavailable was associated with about 50% higher hang-up odds. Greetings ending in a statement rather than a question had about 15% more hang-ups. Greeting length made no difference, and voice gender showed none. The study is non-randomized and the results are associations. Page last updated 16 Jun 2026. [Upfirst](https://upfirst.ai/blog/ai-receptionist-hang-ups)
- **Rime / Miravoice survey-call study (VENDOR-FUNDED; analysis by Miravoice, which held editorial control).** About 99,400 outbound calls to US landlines, Mar-Apr 2026, every call opening with a 20-30 s AI disclosure. Intro hang-up rates were 87.9-90.3% across TTS providers. **No provider differed in the first 10 s**, and early hang-ups were attributed to caller ID and the unsolicited ring rather than the voice. Differences appeared after 25-30 s. The study had no non-disclosure arm, so it says nothing about disclosure as such, but it shows that a long disclosure-led intro on a cold call loses most people. [Rime](https://www.rime.ai/resources/which-ai-voice-keeps-people-on-the-phone)
- **Regal.ai blog, 14 Jan 2025 (VENDOR, no figures).** In an insurance qualification test, changing the timing and framing of the disclosure "roughly doubled" calls that became conversations and transfers. No baseline was given. With disclosure only when asked, 2.7% of education-sector callers asked whether the agent was AI. Up-front disclosure led to faster hang-ups and to "robotic yes/no" answers that treated the agent like an IVR. The control opener combined "automated agent" with "recorded line". The delayed variant named the company and the recorded line, asked "How are you today?", and introduced "As a virtual agent…" in the next turn. [Regal](https://www.regal.ai/blog/ai-agent-ethics-disclosure-timing)
- **Survey (VENDOR).** A 2025 US survey of 2,000 consumers by an answering-service vendor: 37% would lose confidence in a company that hides AI, and 40% would trust more a company that is open about it. Seen only via a search summary. [answeringservicecare.com](https://answeringservicecare.com/insights/robots-reveal-yourself-the-ai-call-report/)
- **India attitudes (SURVEY).** Ipsos AI Monitor 2025 (published 19 Jun 2025, 30-country Global Advisor survey, about 23,000 respondents): **67% of Indians expect products and services that use AI to disclose it** (global figure stated as at least 79%). 66% of Indians are nervous about AI and 65% are excited; India's trust in company AI content runs 10-16 points above the global average. [Ipsos India](https://www.ipsos.com/en-in/trust-and-transparency-emerge-key-cornerstones-ai-usage-among-indians-ipsos-ai-monitor-2025). A 2026 Ipsos release is reported as putting at 83% the share of Indians who want companies *required* to disclose AI use. That figure comes from secondary press only and the wording differs. [MediaBrief](https://mediabrief.com/71-of-indians-believe-ais-societal-benefits-outweigh-its-environmental-costs/)

### Inferences
- The Luo penalty was mostly **an immediate hang-up on a cold disclosure** (56% gone within 5 s). Disclosing after the offer recovered about half the gap (0.048 → 0.110). That points to the first 5 seconds as the risk window, and to what comes immediately after the disclosure as the lever, not to the disclosure itself.
- Three things suggest the penalty shrinks over time: Luo's prior-AI-experience moderator, Habel's null result for LLM-era agents, and Xu's modest 11% response penalty in 2026. India's higher stated trust in company AI (Ipsos) is consistent with a smaller penalty here, though it does not prove one. **No Indian field data was found.**
- Habel's and Xu's results suggest that callers **detect AI anyway**. Volunteering the disclosure then loses little and takes away the "caught hiding it" risk, which matters most for the 67% who expect disclosure.
- Inbound and outbound differ. Upfirst's benefit is for callers who chose to ring a business, where disclosure resolves uncertainty about the line. On outbound, the caller has not chosen the call, so Luo and Rime are the more relevant evidence.

### Gaps
- No published experiment found on Indian-language (Telugu or Hindi) AI calls, and no India vendor A/B data.
- Not found or not verifiable: disclosure A/B data from Retell, Vapi, Bland, PolyAI or Sierra, and Google Duplex's disclosure behaviour from a primary source. Searches surfaced only vendor compliance blogs without data.
- Habel et al.: industry, country, disclosure wording and timing UNKNOWN (SSRN 403). Xu et al.: wording and timing of the disclosure UNKNOWN (full text 403).
- The PwC "54% would hang up" figure and the "29% hang up immediately" figure in circulating blogs could not be traced to primary sources and are excluded.

## 2. Placement: first sentence, after the reason, or only when asked; combining with the recording notice; length

### Takeaway
Calevate's policy (volunteered, plus truthful when asked), the EU rule (at or before the first interaction), and the evidence together point to the same structure. Use one short opening turn: greeting, business name, AI identity and purpose in one breath, a recording notice folded in, and a question at the end. Disclosing only after the pitch improves conversion in Luo, but it is incompatible with "volunteered up front" and with Article 50's timing. It is a design reference only, not an option here.

### Cited Findings
- Luo 2019: disclosure **before** the conversation gave 56% hang-ups within 5 s; disclosure **after** the pitch gave 4.5%. The disclosure was a bare identity sentence with no reason or benefit attached. [PDF](https://www.fox.temple.edu/sites/fox/files/Frontiers-Machines-versus-Humans-The-Impact-of-Artificial-Intelligence-Chatbot-Disclosure-on-Customer-Purchases.pdf)
- EU AI Act Art. 50(5): the information must be given "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure", "in a clear and distinguishable manner", and must meet accessibility requirements. [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Upfirst (inbound, observational): a recording notice was associated with about 30% fewer hang-ups and AI disclosure with about 20% fewer. Greeting length made no difference. Ending on a question beat ending on a statement by about 15%. Leading with "the owner is unavailable" was associated with about 50% more hang-ups. [Upfirst](https://upfirst.ai/blog/ai-receptionist-hang-ups)
- Rime/Miravoice: a 20-30 s disclosure intro on cold landline calls saw roughly 88-90% intro hang-ups, and the first 10 s were dominated by caller ID and ring effects. [Rime](https://www.rime.ai/resources/which-ai-voice-keeps-people-on-the-phone)
- Regal (VENDOR): the variant that named the company and the recorded line, asked "how are you", and disclosed "virtual agent" in the second turn outperformed the opener that disclosed "automated agent" plus "recorded line" up front. No figures were given. [Regal](https://www.regal.ai/blog/ai-agent-ethics-disclosure-timing)
- Utah Code 13-77 (SB 226, eff. 7 May 2025; SECONDARY-LEGAL): clear disclosure at the start of an interaction, and throughout it, earns a safe harbour. [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2025/04/utah-regulation-ai-policy-mental-health-chatbots); [PubLedge](https://publedge.org/requires/us-ut-legislature-statute-2025-sb226/disclose-genai-high-risk-proactive/)

### Inferences (testable recommendations)
- **P1. Put the disclosure in the first turn, attached to the business name and a reason, never alone.** A suggested order is greeting → business → "AI assistant" + persona → why I'm calling (one clause, tied to the person's own inquiry or relationship) → question. This avoids Luo's bare-identity cold open while staying inside "first interaction". Test it against a "disclosure as sentence 2, after the reason" variant. Both are compliant with Calevate's volunteered policy if they fall in the opening turn.
- **P2. Fold the recording notice into the same breath** ("…an AI assistant on a recorded line…"). Upfirst's association and Regal's variants both bundle the two, and a single clause costs fewer seconds than two sentences. Hard rule 5 keeps the two as separately switchable obligations. Combine them only in wording, and only when both toggles are on.
- **P3. Budget about 8-10 s for the opening turn, then hand the floor over with a question.** No source gives an optimal length. This is an inference from Luo's 5-second hang-up window and Rime's finding that a 20-30 s intro loses most cold callees. Measure it as the share of calls under 10 s and under 15 s, by variant.
- **P4. Never open with what the agent cannot do or who is absent** (Upfirst's "owner unavailable" association).
- **P5. Metrics for an A/B (ROADMAP-sized, no new infrastructure):** hang-up ≤5 s after disclosure (Luo's definition), calls under 15 s (Upfirst/Regal), "are you a robot" questions per call, DNC/opt-out requests per call, and conversion to the campaign's success event. Randomize per contact, not per campaign.

### Gaps
- No study isolates "business name first" against "AI first". UNKNOWN.
- No study isolates combining the recording notice with the disclosure against separate sentences on outbound calls. Upfirst covers inbound only and is observational.
- No study gives an optimal opening length in seconds.

## 3. Wording style: "AI assistant" vs "virtual" vs "automated"; persona names; capability honesty; offering a human; handling objections

### Takeaway
No controlled study compares the labels "AI assistant", "virtual assistant" and "automated". The design anchors are these. First, regulators test whether the person is actually informed: "clear and distinguishable" in the EU, and in California a disclosure "reasonably designed to inform", per secondary sources. That favours the plainest term people recognize. Second, the penalty runs through perceived **competence and empathy** (Luo, Habel), so the line after the disclosure should show both: a concrete, honest capability and an easy route to a person.

### Cited Findings
- Penalty mechanism: lower perceived knowledge and empathy (Luo 2019, mediation p<0.01) [PDF](https://www.fox.temple.edu/sites/fox/files/Frontiers-Machines-versus-Humans-The-Impact-of-Artificial-Intelligence-Chatbot-Disclosure-on-Customer-Purchases.pdf). Habel et al. report expected low empathy and insincerity as the reasons people break off, and weak sales-process handling as the reason AI underperforms [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/7013259.pdf?abstractid=7013259&mirid=1).
- Human-like delivery (interjections, fillers) offset the disclosure penalty through trust (Xu, Dai & Yan 2026, abstract) [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/epdf/10.1287/mnsc.2022.03833).
- Upfirst: greetings that listed skills or capabilities were associated with about 30-40% lower hang-up odds (possibly confounded by owner diligence) [Upfirst](https://upfirst.ai/blog/ai-receptionist-hang-ups).
- Regal tested "automated agent", "virtual agent" and "AI-powered agent" plus a named persona ("I'm Danny, a virtual agent…") but published no comparison between labels [Regal](https://www.regal.ai/blog/ai-agent-ethics-disclosure-timing).
- Luo found **no deception effect and no complaints** when disclosure came up front. Their penalty came from bias against machines, not from feeling tricked [PDF](https://www.fox.temple.edu/sites/fox/files/Frontiers-Machines-versus-Humans-The-Impact-of-Artificial-Intelligence-Chatbot-Disclosure-on-Customer-Purchases.pdf).

### Inferences (testable recommendations)
- **W1. Say "AI" explicitly** ("AI assistant", or the language's plain equivalent). "Virtual" and "automated" are weaker under any "reasonably well-informed person" or "reasonably designed to inform" test, and "automated" invites the IVR frame Regal describes. In Telugu and Hindi speech "AI" is widely used as an English loanword, but whether rural callers understand it is UNKNOWN. Test "AI assistant" against a native-language gloss.
- **W2. A persona name is fine, as long as "AI" sits right next to it in the same clause** ("<Name>, <Business>'s AI assistant"). A name with no "AI" next to it reads as a human claim.
- **W3. Follow the disclosure immediately with one concrete competence line plus the human handoff**, for example: what I can do now, and what a person at <Business> will confirm. This targets the knowledge and empathy mediators directly. The handoff must be real. Promise only what the agent's tools and the client's staff actually deliver (hard rule 11 applies to the agent's claims too).
- **W4. When a caller objects ("I don't want to talk to a machine"):** acknowledge without arguing, offer the alternative straight away (a callback from a person, or the business's number), record the preference, and end politely. Do not try to persuade. Habel's finding that low expected sincerity drives disengagement means pushing back makes it worse. The preference should feed the client's CRM ("prefers human"), and a request not to be called again should feed DNC.
- **W5. Truthful answer when asked mid-call:** a plain "yes, I'm an AI assistant for <Business>", then a return to the caller's goal. This is already enforced server-side by hard rule 5.

### Gaps
- No experiment compares "AI assistant", "virtual assistant", "digital assistant" and "automated" labels, in any language. UNKNOWN.
- No data on whether persona names help or hurt after disclosure.
- No data on how well callers accept a human-handoff offer, or what it does to conversion.

## 4. Regulation and standards elsewhere (design references) and India

### Takeaway
India has no in-call AI disclosure duty on business voice calls. TRAI's 2026 TCCCPR amendment requires **pre-declaring AI/artificial-voice (A2P) calls to the access provider**, not to the called person. MeitY's IT Rules 2026 require a "prominently prefixed audio disclosure" for synthetic audio, but they bind **intermediaries**, and whether they cover a live AI phone call is unresolved. Abroad, the EU's Article 50(1) is the closest analogue and has applied since 2 Aug 2026. US federal law regulates *consent* for AI voices, not disclosure wording. US state laws are narrow: California covers online bots, and Utah applies on request or to high-risk regulated occupations.

### Cited Findings
- **EU AI Act Art. 50(1),(5) (PRIMARY-LEGAL text via artificialintelligenceact.eu):** providers must design systems that interact with people so that they are informed they are dealing with AI, unless that is obvious to a reasonably well-informed person. This must happen at the latest at first interaction, clearly and distinguishably, and accessibly. It applies from **2 Aug 2026**. [Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
  - The Digital Omnibus deferred high-risk duties (Annex III to 2 Dec 2027) but **not** the Art. 50(1) interaction disclosure. Only the 50(2) machine-readable marking got a grace period, to 2 Dec 2026, for systems placed on the market before 2 Aug 2026. (SECONDARY-LEGAL) [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/). The Omnibus regulation number and entry-into-force date differ between sources (one cites Reg. (EU) 2026/1744, 27 Jul 2026). Treat these as REPORTED.
- **US FCC Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17, CG Docket 23-362, released 8 Feb 2024 (SECONDARY-LEGAL):** AI-generated voices are "artificial" voices under the TCPA, so the TCPA's consent rules apply (prior express written consent for telemarketing robocalls). The ruling clarifies existing rules rather than adding a disclosure script. [Cooley](https://cooley.com/news/insight/2024/2024-02-15-fcc-ai-generated-robocalls-illegal-under-the-tcpa); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/fcc-declares-authority-and-intent-to-regulate-ai-generated-calls-under-the-tcpa). The existing TCPA artificial or prerecorded voice identification rules (47 CFR 64.1200(b)) were not re-read here. UNKNOWN as to exact wording.
- **California B&P Code §17940-17943 (SB 1001, 2018):** primary text not read (leginfo and Justia returned 403). From prior knowledge only, and therefore UNVERIFIED: the law targets bots communicating **online** with intent to mislead about their artificial identity in order to incentivize a sale or influence a vote, and a clear, conspicuous disclosure reasonably designed to inform is a defence. Whether it reaches phone calls: UNKNOWN, not established here.
- **Utah Code 13-77 (SB 226, eff. 7 May 2025; SECONDARY-LEGAL):** in consumer transactions, disclosure is required when a person makes a "clear and unambiguous" request to know whether they are dealing with AI. Regulated occupations must disclose proactively at the outset of **high-risk** interactions (those collecting sensitive data or giving personalized advice relied on for significant decisions). Clear disclosure at the start and throughout earns a safe harbour. Fines up to $2,500 per violation are reported. [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2025/04/utah-regulation-ai-policy-mental-health-chatbots); [PubLedge](https://publedge.org/requires/us-ut-legislature-statute-2025-sb226/disclose-genai-high-risk-proactive/)
- **India: MeitY IT (Intermediary Guidelines…) Amendment Rules 2026, G.S.R. 120(E), notified 10 Feb 2026, commencing 20 Feb 2026 (SECONDARY-LEGAL).** Synthetically generated information (SGI) covers audio made to appear real and indistinguishable from a natural person. Audio SGI needs a "prominently prefixed audio disclosure". The obligations fall on **intermediaries**, and text-only content is excluded. Khaitan's summary does not address telephony, and a live AI voice call "could arguably" fit the SGI definition, but application to a business deploying a voice agent is unaddressed. [Khaitan & Co](https://www.khaitanco.com/thought-leadership/MeitY-notifies-the-IT-Amendment-Rules-2026); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/2026-it-rules-amendments-ai-content-labelling-and-intermediary-obligations)
- **India: TRAI TCCCPR (Third Amendment) Regulations 2026, reported as notified 18 Sep 2026 (REPORTED, per repo evidence note).** A2P calls are defined to include "artificial voice". Entities must **pre-declare A2P use and their CLIs to the access provider**, and undeclared A2P calls count as UCC. No in-call disclosure to the called person is reported. [docs/evidence/trai-tcccpr-third-amendment-2026-09-18.md](../../../evidence/trai-tcccpr-third-amendment-2026-09-18.md) (Vobiz summary relayed 26 Sep 2026; Gazette text not read). The press coverage dated 14 Sep 2026 also describes declaration to operators, not to consumers. [Communications Today](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/trai-to-require-disclosure-of-automated-ai-calls-under-anti-spam-rules/). Vendor blogs claiming a "mandatory AI disclosure" in TRAI rules conflict with this and are not primary, so they are excluded. [qcall.ai](https://qcall.ai/trai-updates-for-ai-calling) is one such claim, unverified.

### Inferences
- **Which of these bind a Calevate voice call?** EU Art. 50 binds only if a call reaches people in the EU or the system is placed on the EU market (not the current scope). US FCC and state laws bind only US calls. TRAI's A2P declaration binds Calevate's outbound but is an operator-side declaration. The IT Rules 2026 audio-prefix duty binding live calls is UNKNOWN and is a question for counsel. Volunteering disclosure in the first turn would, however, satisfy a "prefixed audio disclosure" reading if one were ever applied.
- As the strictest live standard, **EU Art. 50 is the right design reference**: first interaction, clear, distinguishable, accessible. The default-on policy already meets it. Keeping the disclosure in the first turn and in plain words (W1) keeps Calevate ahead of any Indian rule that copies it.

### Gaps
- UK: no AI-call disclosure guidance was found in this pass (Ofcom, ICO, CMA). UNKNOWN.
- California SB 1001's primary text was not read (403), so its scope over voice is unverified.
- TRAI Gazette text and any in-call AI provision were not read (trai.gov.in is egress-blocked per the repo evidence note). Any pending TRAI consultation on synthetic voice is UNKNOWN beyond vendor forecasts.
- No MeitY advisory specific to AI voice calls was found.

## 5. Cultural notes for India: politeness forms, call-centre expectations, mixed language

### Takeaway
No sourced research was found on how Indian callers respond to AI disclosure phrasing, honorifics or code-mixing in voice calls. Everything below is design inference to be tested, not evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Indians report both higher excitement (65%) and higher nervousness (66%) about AI than the global average, and higher trust in companies' AI content (44-47% vs 29-34% globally) [Ipsos India](https://www.ipsos.com/en-in/trust-and-transparency-emerge-key-cornerstones-ai-usage-among-indians-ipsos-ai-monitor-2025).
- Prior AI experience reduces the disclosure penalty (Luo 2019) [PDF](https://www.fox.temple.edu/sites/fox/files/Frontiers-Machines-versus-Humans-The-Impact-of-Artificial-Intelligence-Chatbot-Disclosure-on-Customer-Purchases.pdf).

### Inferences (to test; not evidence)
- Honorific address (Telugu "-గారు" or "అండి" endings, Hindi "ji" or "aap") is the conventional register for business calls in these languages. Applying it in the disclosure turn targets the empathy mediator in Luo and Habel. Test honorific against neutral register in the opening turn.
- Leaving "AI" as an English loanword inside a Telugu or Hindi sentence (W1) matches common urban code-mixing. For less-urban audiences, test a gloss in the native language (for example "computer assistant") next to it. Comprehension is UNKNOWN.
- Callers used to scripted call-centre openers ("calling from <company> regarding…") may react better when the AI disclosure follows that familiar frame than to a novel opener. Test this under P1.
- Hard rule: the founder writes the Telugu lines. Nothing here should be copied from a competitor's script.

### Gaps
- No academic or survey source found on Indian (Telugu, Hindi or English) caller reactions to AI disclosure, honorifics or code-mixing in AI calls. UNKNOWN; the only source would be Calevate's own A/B under P5.
- Not found: India-specific consumer data on preferring a human agent over AI in phone service.
