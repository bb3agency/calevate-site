# Sales call craft for short warm outbound and inbound qualification calls (AI voice agents, Indian SMBs, real estate first)

Read date for every source below: 30 Oct 2026 unless stated. Evidence classes:
- **PEER-REVIEWED**: published academic study, read at least at abstract or index level.
- **VENDOR-DATA**: a vendor's own analysis of its own dataset (Gong, Truecaller). Real data, but not independently audited, and usually US B2B.
- **VENDOR-GUIDANCE**: a vendor's how-to (Vapi, Retell, Sell.do, NoBroker). This is opinion, not measurement.
- **SECONDARY**: a blog or news summary of someone else's data. Not verified against the original.
- **OPINION**: practitioner advice with no data.

**The main caveat on transfer:** nearly all the quantitative sales-call research found here is US or English-language B2B (Gong, InsideSales/XANT, HBR). None of it measures Indian B2C real-estate calls, Telugu calls, or AI agents. Treat every number as directional for Calevate, not a benchmark.

## 1. Openers for warm callbacks: permission check, reason for the call, name check, first 10 seconds, speed to lead

### Takeaway
The best data supports **saying plainly why you are calling** (about 2.1x the baseline in Gong's cold-call data). It does not support a "Did I catch you at a bad time?" permission opener, which did worse than baseline. Speed to lead (calling within minutes of an enquiry) has the strongest evidence of anything in this file. The famous "21x" figure does trace to a real 2007 study (Oldroyd, with InsideSales/XANT), but it was vendor-linked and its sample sizes conflict across retellings.

### Cited findings
- **Reason for the call.** In Gong's analysis of 90,380 cold calls (first interactions only), "The reason for my call is…" gave about **2.1x the success rate** of the 1.5% baseline. Success appears to mean a booked meeting, though the page does not formally define it. Author Chris Orlob, published 19 Apr 2018. The page attributes the idea to John Barrows and says the reason "doesn't need to be strong, only present". Class: VENDOR-DATA. — [Gong Labs, cold call opening lines](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/cold-call-opening-lines/)
- **The permission opener hurt.** "Did I catch you at a bad time?" had a **0.9%** success rate against the 1.5% baseline, about 40% less likely to succeed. Class: VENDOR-DATA. — [Gong Labs, cold call opening lines](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/cold-call-opening-lines/)
- **Social openers.** "How have you been?" scored 10.01% (6.6x) and some version of "How are you?" scored 5.2% (3.4x). The author calls "How have you been?" a pattern interrupt, but the study did not test that claim, and it is his opinion. The page also says delivery matters more than the words and that a robotic tone undermines the line (OPINION). — [Gong Labs, cold call opening lines](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/cold-call-opening-lines/)
- **A later Gong analysis contradicts this one.** A secondary summary of a 2024 Gong re-analysis of about 300M calls reports a "heard the name tossed around" opener at 11.24% and a permission-based opener at **11.18%**. This contradicts the 2018 finding on permission openers. I could not open the Gong article (the URL resolved to the Gong homepage), so this is SECONDARY and unverified. — [Gong "best and worst cold call openers" (did not resolve)](https://gong.io/?p=53640), relayed via the search summary; also cited by [Prospeo, sales openers (SECONDARY)](https://prospeo.io/s/sales-openers)
- **Speed to lead: the original is HBR 2011.** "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" by James B. Oldroyd, Kristina McElheran and David Elkington, *Harvard Business Review* 89(3):28 (2011). Class: PEER-REVIEWED in the loose sense (HBR is an editorial outlet, not a refereed journal). — [BYU ScholarsArchive record](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9711)
  - According to secondary coverage, the HBR study audited **2,241 US companies** with a test web lead, and a companion analysis covered **1.25M leads from 29 B2C and 13 B2B companies**. — [SmartCompany, 4 Jul 2011 (SECONDARY)](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/marketing/20110704-are-you-letting-online-leads-go-cold/)
  - Also via secondary coverage: only 37% of companies responded to online leads within an hour. — [Entrepreneur (SECONDARY)](https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/how-to-maximize-your-online-sales-leads/272927)
- **"21x within 5 minutes": the original has been found, but not read.** It comes from the 2007 Lead Response Management study (Oldroyd with InsideSales/XANT), presented at the MarketingSherpa B2B Demand Summit in October 2007. The claim: contacting a lead within 5 minutes made it **21x more likely to qualify** than contacting it at 30 minutes, and gave **100x** better odds of reaching the lead at all. — [InsideSales press page (VENDOR, the study's commercial sponsor)](https://www.insidesales.com/xant-com-research-stuns-2007-marketing-sherpa-b2b-summit); [RC Reader (SECONDARY)](https://www.rcreader.com/node/19257)
  - **Conflicts.** Retellings give the sample as "more than 1 million leads from 38 companies" or "15,000 leads and 100,000 call attempts". Oldroyd's affiliation is given as MIT Sloan or Kellogg. I did not read the original 2007 report. Class: **REPORTED, original not read, sponsor-linked**.
  - One retelling notes "dramatic variations in optimal response time according to industry sector". — [InsideSales (VENDOR)](https://insidesales.com/?p=5445)
  - Figures such as "80% drop in the first 5 minutes" could not be traced to the original. Do not use them.
- **Indian real estate, opinion only.** NoBroker's blog advises developers to respond to enquiries quickly and to offer convenient appointment slots. Class: VENDOR-GUIDANCE/OPINION. — [NoBrokerHood, increase site visits](https://www.nobrokerhood.com/blog/how-to-increase-site-visits-in-real-estate/)

### Inferences
- For Calevate's warm callback, a **plain reason for the call that names what the lead enquired about** is the opener best supported by evidence. Gong found a reason works even when it is weak, and in Calevate's case the reason is true and strong ("you enquired about 2BHK flats in Kokapet").
- A **"Is now a good time?" check after the reason**, not instead of it, is defensible on courtesy and complaint-risk grounds. Gong's 2018 data says a permission check *as the opener* depressed bookings, but that was US B2B cold calling. The 2024 data reportedly reverses it, and a warm, consented callback is a different situation. The order "name → who I am → reason → quick time check" is a design choice, not a measured optimum.
- "How have you been?" implies an earlier relationship. An AI agent on a first contact would be implying something false, so it conflicts with Calevate's honesty constraints. Do not borrow it.
- Speed to lead is the most robust lever, and it is operational (when the call fires), not wording. A callback within minutes of a form fill is well supported in direction, though the exact multipliers are not.

### Gaps
- The text of the 2007 Lead Response Management report and the HBR 2011 article was not read (paywalled, and not found in open form). The 21x and 100x figures are REPORTED.
- The 2024 Gong 300M-call opener article could not be retrieved, so the reversal on permission openers is unconfirmed.
- No data was found on openers for **warm, inbound-originated callbacks** specifically, or on Indian or Telugu calls.
- Name check ("Am I speaking with Ravi garu?"): no study found. It is standard practice (OPINION) and also a privacy safeguard against disclosing an enquiry to the wrong person.

## 2. Qualification frameworks for 1-3 minute SMB calls; question count; one question per turn

### Takeaway
No study measures BANT, CHAMP or MEDDIC on short B2C calls. The only quantitative signal is Gong's: won deals averaged **15-16 questions against about 20 on lost deals**, on calls of 10 minutes or more. That suggests over-questioning correlates with losing. Voice-AI vendor guidance converges on **one question per turn and one or two sentences per reply**.

### Cited findings
- **Question count.** Won deals averaged **15-16 questions** and lost deals about **20**, in a dataset of **326K calls of at least 10 minutes**, updated 20 Mar 2025. Author Dan Morgese, published 21 Aug 2025, modified 4 Mar 2026. Class: VENDOR-DATA, B2B. — [Gong Labs, talk-to-listen ratio](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio/)
- **Short monologues.** Lost deals "often feature long seller monologues". No length is given. — same source.
- **One question per turn.** Vapi: "Ask only one question at a time". Collect one field, confirm it, move on. "Keep responses to one or two sentences maximum." When listing options, offer two and ask whether the caller wants more. Class: VENDOR-GUIDANCE. — [Vapi Voice AI Prompting Guide](https://docs.vapi.ai/prompting-guide)
- **Read-backs.** Vapi: read back transactional data (appointment times, spellings), but **skip read-backs for soft data** such as interest level or timeline. "Batch confirmation at the end." On a correction, "update only that field". — [Vapi Prompting Guide](https://docs.vapi.ai/prompting-guide)
- **Match the stage.** Sell.do: "a first-time inquirer doesn't need an invitation to a site visit—they need clarity on project highlights and location." Class: VENDOR-GUIDANCE (Indian real-estate CRM). — [Sell.do, customer journey touchpoints](https://www.sell.do/blog/identify-and-optimize-customer-journey-touchpoints-in-real-estate)

### Inferences
- Scaled from Gong's 10-minute-plus calls to a 1-3 minute call, the useful version is "ask only what changes the next step". For real estate: location/area → configuration (BHK) → budget band → timeline → (financing or decision-maker only if they bear on the visit) → visit slot. That is about **4-6 questions** before the close. This is an inference from the direction of Gong's data and Vapi's guidance, not a measured ceiling.
- Fold questions together when the lead has already answered something (for example, the form already gave the BHK). Confirming beats re-asking ("You were looking at 2BHK, right?").
- BANT's "Authority" maps naturally to "Will family be joining the visit?". That can be asked as logistics rather than as a gatekeeping question (OPINION).

### Gaps
- No evidence found on question fatigue thresholds for calls under 3 minutes, or for B2C or Indian callers.
- The "11-14 questions" figure circulating on third-party sites was not found on Gong's own pages, so it is UNVERIFIED.

## 3. Value statements: a short, true value line without overclaiming; social proof

### Takeaway
No direct study was found on value lines in short qualification calls. The evidence-backed constraints are regulatory (India's TRAI rule that mixing promotional content into a service call makes the whole call promotional) and stylistic (short replies, no monologue). A value line should therefore be a **factual, lead-specific statement tied to what they enquired about**, not a pitch.

### Cited findings
- **TRAI mixing rule (India).** The TCCCPR Second Amendment (notified 12 Feb 2025) revised the definitions of promotional, service and transactional communications. It limits service calls to existing customers on inferred consent and extends "promotional" to calls to prospective customers. **If promotional content is mixed into a service or transactional communication, the whole communication is treated as promotional.** It also introduced the 140 series for promotional calls and the 1600 series for service/transactional calls. Class: SECONDARY (law-firm summaries; TRAI's own PDF was unreachable, connection refused from this host). — [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leadership/Efforts-to-Curb-Spam-Continue-TRAI-Releases-Second-Amendment-to-the-TCCCPR-2018); [JSA Prism Feb 2025](https://www.jsalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/JSA-Prism-Telecommunications_February-2025_TCCCPR-amendments.Final_.pdf); [TRAI PR 11/2025 (not retrieved)](https://cms.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/PR_No.11of2025.pdf)
- **Long monologues lose.** Lost deals feature long seller monologues (Gong, as above). — [Gong Labs](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio/)
- **Price is the top hesitation.** Sell.do says price is the biggest hesitation in Indian real estate (OPINION). — [Sell.do, channel partner strategies](https://www.sell.do/blog/top-strategies-to-improve-real-estate-sales-for-indian-channel-partners)

### Inferences
- **Value line pattern (structure, not a slogan):** [one configured fact about the project or business] + [why it is relevant to what *this* lead just said]. For example, after the lead says "Gachibowli, 2BHK, under 90 lakh", the agent states one configured fact that matches (location, possession date, configuration availability), then moves on. Where it sits matters: **after qualification, before the close**, so it reads as an answer to their need and not as an opening pitch.
- Every fact must come from the business's configured knowledge, with no adjectives the business did not supply ("best", "cheapest", "guaranteed appreciation"). Earnings or appreciation claims are excluded by the founder's constraint, and are a known regulatory-risk area for real-estate advertising (UNVERIFIED this session; see Gaps).
- **Social proof** ("many families from your area have visited"): include it only if the business has configured a true, specific statement. Invented social proof is a false claim. No study was found on social proof in phone qualification.
- **Calevate design question, not settled by this research:** whether a value line on a warm callback tips the call into "promotional" under the mixing rule. That is a legal call. The safest reading is to keep it informational and responsive to what the lead asked (project facts in answer to their stated need), not discount offers or scheme pitches.

### Gaps
- No study found on whether a value statement raises booking rates on short qualification calls. UNKNOWN.
- No primary TRAI text was read for where "information requested by the customer" ends and "promotional" begins on a voice call. Route to legal review.
- RERA: whether a call must state the project's RERA registration number (the RERA Act requires it on advertisements) was **not verified this session**. Calling it a requirement on calls would be a guess. Check the RERA Act and the Telangana RERA rules directly.

## 4. Closes: assumptive vs two-choice vs direct ask; confirmation and repeat-back; honest expectations

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed study was found comparing the "alternative close" with a direct ask in sales or scheduling. The adjacent evidence is mixed. Freedom-affirming language ("but you are free to say no") looked strong in a 2013 meta-analysis, but a 2023 bias-corrected re-analysis found **no effect** in the low-risk studies. The strongest related evidence is on **implementation intentions**: asking people to commit to a specific **date and time** measurably raised follow-through (PNAS 2011). That directly supports a concrete slot plus a repeat-back.

### Cited findings
- **Date-and-time planning prompt.** Milkman, Beshears, Choi, Laibson and Madrian, *PNAS* 108(26):10415-10420 (2011): a randomized field trial on 3,272 employees. A mailing prompting people to write down a planned vaccination **date and time** raised vaccination **4.2 percentage points** over a 33.1% control, which was significant. A **date-only** prompt gave +1.5 pp, which was **not significant**. The effect was larger where the clinic ran on a single day. Class: PEER-REVIEWED. — [PMC3127912](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3127912); [NBER w17183](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17183/w17183.pdf)
- **"But you are free" (BYAF).** Carpenter (2013, *Communication Studies*) meta-analysis: 42 studies, about 22,000 participants, compliance roughly doubled. These figures come from secondary write-ups; the original paper was not read. — [UMD DRUM record](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/handle/1903/24608)
- **BYAF re-analysis.** Fillon, Souchet, Pascual and Girandola, *Meta-Psychology* (2023), pre-registered: 52 studies, N = 28,759, g = 0.44 overall. But only **7 studies were low risk of bias, and they showed no effect (g = 0.11, CI −0.18 to 0.40)**, with possible publication bias. Class: PEER-REVIEWED. — [Meta-Psychology](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2640)
- **Choice and control.** A 2021 *Psychological Science* paper (17 experiments) found that choice rarely makes people feel more likely to get preferable outcomes unless it actually makes them more likely. Class: PEER-REVIEWED, indirect. — [Psychological Science 0956797620958009](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/0956797620958009/)
- **Vapi on read-backs and options.** Read back appointment times; offer two options at once; batch confirmation at the end. Class: VENDOR-GUIDANCE. — [Vapi Prompting Guide](https://docs.vapi.ai/prompting-guide)
- **Reminders and no-shows (healthcare).** Results are mixed: some studies report large reductions, others none or modest. Class: PEER-REVIEWED review. — [Journal of Research in Medical Sciences 2023, via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/2e1444ce32f04b8695001cabde469591)
- **Vendor no-show figures are unverified.** "Automated WhatsApp reminders cut site-visit no-shows by up to 40%" is an unverified vendor claim. — [Groweon (VENDOR)](https://www.groweon.com/blog/?p=1588)
- **Follow-up on missed visits.** Indian presales roles routinely include following up on scheduled, missed and pending site visits. Class: OPINION/practice. — [Weekday job listing, Disha Habitat](https://jobs.weekday.works/senior-presales-consultant-at-disha-habitat-wkdy6uo7pb)

### Inferences
- A **two-choice close works best as two *real*, configured slots** ("Saturday 11 am or Sunday 4 pm?"). That is both the alternative-close shape and a concrete date-and-time commitment, the specific form Milkman found effective (date-only was not). Its value comes from *making the plan concrete*, not from a manipulative "illusion of choice"; no evidence was found for the latter.
- Always leave an exit ("or another time that suits you better?"). This keeps the close honest and non-coercive. BYAF evidence is too weak to justify it as a *conversion* tactic, but it is consistent with the no-pressure constraint.
- **Repeat-back is mandatory** for the date, time and place, done once at the end (Vapi). Speak numbers in spoken form.
- **Next-step clarity:** state only what will actually happen, as configured. "Our team will call to confirm" is acceptable only if that is the business's real process. "I'll send the location on WhatsApp" is acceptable only if a WhatsApp action exists. Promising an unconfigured action breaks trust and the founder's rule. No study is needed for this: it is a truthfulness constraint.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found on alternative close vs direct ask vs assumptive close for booking rates. UNKNOWN; all claims about it are OPINION.
- No Gong-primary data found on "next steps" and win rates. The search returned only product pages.

## 5. Objection handling: price, timing, "send details on WhatsApp", "I'm busy", "already bought", "who gave my number"

### Takeaway
The data-backed technique is **pause and acknowledge before answering**. Gong measured a "patience score": top reps pause longer after an objection. The often-quoted "5x longer" figure appears only in third-party write-ups. Gong also advises against asking "why" and suggests mirroring the buyer's last words. The rest is practitioner opinion. For compliance-sensitive objections ("who gave my number", "stop calling"), the answer is set by law and policy, not by sales craft.

### Cited findings
- **Patience score.** Gong measured "the average length star reps pause after getting an objection, compared to their peers" and calls it the Patience Score. Its advice: mirror the buyer's last few words with an upward tone, then pause; avoid "why", which can feel threatening. Class: VENDOR-DATA plus VENDOR-GUIDANCE. — [Gong Labs, handling sales objections](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/handling-sales-objections/)
- **Unverified multipliers.** "Successful reps pause 5x longer" (from 67,149 calls) and "reps speed up from 173 to 188 wpm under objection" appear in third-party summaries only, not confirmed on Gong's page. — [Prospeo (SECONDARY)](https://prospeo.io/s/objection-handling); [Hyperbound (SECONDARY)](https://www.hyperbound.ai/blog/how-to-handle-sales-objections)
- **Price.** Price is the biggest hesitation in Indian real estate (Sell.do, OPINION). — [Sell.do](https://www.sell.do/blog/top-strategies-to-improve-real-estate-sales-for-indian-channel-partners)
- **Interruptions and unclear speech.** Vapi: "The caller interrupts you. Stop talking, listen, respond." For unclear input, ask the caller to repeat; after two failures, offer a transfer. "Do not apologize or attempt to guess." — [Vapi Prompting Guide](https://docs.vapi.ai/prompting-guide)

### Inferences (patterns, OPINION-grade, built on the constraints above)
- **"I'm busy":** acknowledge, then offer a configured callback time as a two-choice and end quickly. Do not push the pitch.
- **"Send details on WhatsApp":** if a WhatsApp send action is configured, confirm the number and say what will be sent. If not, say honestly what *can* happen (for example, "our team can share them"), but only if that is the configured process. Optionally ask one light question so the details sent are relevant. Never claim a send that will not happen.
- **Price ("too costly"):** acknowledge, ask the budget band if not already known, and state configured price-range facts if they exist. If not, offer the visit or team call as where exact pricing is discussed. No discounts unless configured. No appreciation or return claims.
- **Timing ("not now, maybe 6 months"):** accept it, ask whether a later check-in is okay, record it. No false urgency ("only 2 units left") unless the business configured that as a verified fact.
- **"Already bought":** congratulate, close politely, and mark the lead so it is not re-called.
- **"Who gave my number?":** answer truthfully from the lead's source record (for example, the enquiry form on the business's site or portal, and the date). If the source is unknown, say so and offer to stop calling. If they ask to stop, honour it immediately (do-not-call). The underlying mechanism is the spam-complaint risk in section 8; this is a compliance answer, not a persuasion opportunity.
- **"Are you a robot / AI?":** answer truthfully (a Calevate hard rule). No evidence was found on how disclosure affects conversion; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- No primary data on objection frequency or handling outcomes in Indian B2C calls.
- No evidence found on how AI disclosure affects conversion or satisfaction. UNKNOWN.

## 6. Talk ratio, pace, call length, question count, listening signals

### Takeaway
Gong's 2025 update: **won calls had about 57% rep talk time and lost calls about 62%**. Its famous 2016 "43:57 golden ratio" is now historical. Talking more than 65% of a call was linked to lower win rates. These are B2B calls of 10 minutes or more. Translated to a 2-minute AI call, the direction is the same: **the agent should talk less than half the time**.

### Cited findings
- **Talk ratios.** All calls averaged 60% talk / 40% listen. Won deals 57%; lost deals 62%. Low performers swung from 54% (won) to 64% (lost). Over 65% was linked to lower conversion (from 2016). Dataset: 326K calls of at least 10 minutes, data dated 20 Mar 2025. The 2016 original was by Chris Orlob. Class: VENDOR-DATA. — [Gong Labs, talk-to-listen ratio](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio/)
- **Questions.** Won 15-16, lost about 20 (same source).
- **Pause.** Gong advises a 2-second pause before responding, but reports no measured outcome for it (OPINION). — same source.
- **Reply length.** Voice-agent guidance: one or two sentences per reply; on silence, wait 10-15 seconds, check in once, and end only if there is still no response. Class: VENDOR-GUIDANCE. — [Vapi Prompting Guide](https://docs.vapi.ai/prompting-guide)
- **Buyer silence.** Gong Labs published "Silence is golden, unless it's your buyer" (120M+ calls, 9 Oct 2026). The content was not retrieved; only the teaser was seen. — [Gong homepage teaser](https://gong.io/?p=53640)

### Inferences
- Target well under 50% agent talk on a qualification call. The agent's turns are short questions; the lead's answers are the substance.
- Listening signals the agent can produce in speech: brief acknowledgements ("got it", "okay") before the next question; reflecting their key words back ("Gachibowli, 2BHK — okay"); never talking over them (the turn-taking / barge-in setting is an engine concern).

### Gaps
- No benchmark found for total call length on successful short B2C qualification calls. UNKNOWN.
- No data on speaking pace (words per minute) for Telugu or Indian-English calls.

## 7. Real-estate (India) specifics: site visits, weekends, family decisions, NRI buyers, loan pre-approval, RERA

### Takeaway
Reliable quantitative benchmarks (lead-to-visit ratio, weekend share, visit-to-booking) were **not found**. Indian practitioner sources stress fast response, convenient slots, stage-appropriate pitching and follow-up on missed visits. NRI loan mechanics are well documented by lenders. In-principle pre-approval before choosing a property, and a co-applicant (usually family), are relevant to qualification.

### Cited findings
- **Visits.** Respond quickly, offer convenient slots, keep the property presentable (VENDOR-GUIDANCE). — [NoBrokerHood](https://www.nobrokerhood.com/blog/how-to-increase-site-visits-in-real-estate/)
- **Stage.** First-time inquirers need clarity on highlights and location rather than an immediate visit invitation (VENDOR-GUIDANCE). — [Sell.do](https://www.sell.do/blog/identify-and-optimize-customer-journey-touchpoints-in-real-estate)
- **The visit itself.** "Don't just show the project—plan the experience" (VENDOR-GUIDANCE). — [Sell.do channel partner strategies](https://www.sell.do/blog/top-strategies-to-improve-real-estate-sales-for-indian-channel-partners)
- **NRI pre-approval.** NRIs can get in-principle approval before selecting a property. Lenders state this (source class: lender). — [IIFL Home Loans, NRI steps](https://beta.iiflhomeloans.com/blogs/step-step-guide-applying-nri-home-loan); [DBS NRI home loan](https://www.dbs.com/in/treasures/nri-banking/nri-loans/nri-home-loan)
- **NRI co-applicant.** A co-applicant (often an earning immediate family member) is described as mandatory for NRI home loans. — search summary citing [IIFL](https://iiflhomeloans.com/blogs/things-to-know-before-taking-an-nri-home-loan); not opened directly, so SECONDARY
- **NRI site visits.** NRIs often struggle to visit frequently; remote monitoring matters. — [South Indian Bank NRI newsletter, Sep 2023](https://www.southindianbank.com/UserFiles/file/NRI_News_letter_September_2023.pdf)
- **NRI share of sales.** Developers attribute roughly a quarter of premium-segment sales to non-resident buyers. Class: SECONDARY news; not verified. — [Eastern Eye](https://www.easterneye.biz/?p=173807)

### Inferences
- **Family decision-maker:** ask "Who will be coming for the visit?". It qualifies authority and also sets up a slot that suits the family. Weekend slots are the obvious default for working families, but **no data on weekend share was found**; let the business configure its slots.
- **NRI leads:** a site visit may be impossible. The relevant next step may be a video walkthrough or a call with family in India, but only if the business has configured it. Ask about timezone before proposing a callback slot.
- **Financing:** "Will you be taking a home loan?" is a reasonable qualification question. The agent should not give loan advice or eligibility estimates (that edges toward financial advice and outcome claims).

### Gaps
- Lead→visit and visit→booking ratios in Indian residential real estate: UNKNOWN (no disclosure found).
- RERA number on calls: not verified (see section 3 gaps).
- Telangana / Hyderabad-specific buyer behaviour: no source found.

## 8. What not to do in AI voice sales: aggressive tactics, pressure, false urgency; evidence of harm

### Takeaway
The harm evidence is **regulatory and spam-tagging**, not academic. India has very high spam volume, with sales/telemarketing the largest category. TRAI's 2025 amendment tightened complaint-triggered action against senders. The US treats AI voices as "artificial voice" requiring consent. Pressure tactics feed the complaint channel that gets numbers blocked.

### Cited findings
- **Spam volume.** Truecaller 2025 India Insights: about **4,168 crore (41.68B) spam calls** in India in 2025; about 1,189 crore blocked; **66% of unknown calls** to Truecaller users identified as spam; India ranked 5th most spam-affected. Sales/telemarketing was **36%**. Outlets disagree on the denominator (all spam vs fraud calls). Class: VENDOR-DATA via SECONDARY news. — [Business Today, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/india/story/indians-received-over-4000-crore-spam-calls-in-2025-1189-crore-blocked-report-515765-2026-02-11); [Deccan Herald](https://deccanherald.com/technology/truecaller-report-india-hit-by-4168-crore-spam-calls-in-2025-3896311)
- **AI voice calls.** Truecaller warns that AI-generated voice calls are getting harder to distinguish from real ones and that AI-voice scams may rise in 2026. No quantified AI share. — same sources
- **Complaint-triggered action.** The TRAI 2025 amendment brought stricter rules and faster complaint resolution; reporting says 5 complaints can trigger action against a sender. ### Inferences
- **Do not use:** false scarcity or urgency ("only today", "last 2 units") unless it is a configured, verified fact. Repeated pushes after a "no" (one respectful re-offer at most). Implied prior relationships ("How have you been?"). Promises of actions that are not configured. Returns or appreciation claims. Discounts or schemes on a service call (mixing rule). Dodging "Are you AI?" or "Who gave my number?".
- Each of these raises the chance of a spam report or complaint. That is the mechanism that, under TRAI 2025, can lead to action against the sender, and through caller-ID tagging can lower pickup rates for every later call from that number. The causal link to Calevate's own pickup rate is an inference; no Calevate data exists yet.
