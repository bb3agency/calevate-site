## Framing

This report settles, to the extent primary sources allow, which entity may lawfully hold a business telecom connection used by an AI voice-agent SaaS, and compares Model A (platform-owned numbers, allocated to clients) against Model B (client-owned numbers, platform as Telemarketer/API operator). Confidence labels (CONFIRMED / REPORTED / UNRESOLVED) are given per claim.

## Part 1 — Model A: platform holds the connections

**1.1 Reseller CAF obligation — UNRESOLVED as stated, but strongly adverse to Model A.** DoT's business-connection KYC instructions (effective October 1, 2023, expanded May 20, 2024) place the KYC obligation on the **licensee** — the access-service or UL(VNO) operator — to collect the end-customer's CIN/business licence, address, GST certificate, and end-user list before issuing a business connection. The instructions are written as obligations on licensees toward whoever is issued the connection; they are silent on a non-licensee reseller sitting between the licensee and the ultimate user. No primary source found addresses whether a SaaS reseller furnishing its *own* documents (rather than the client's) discharges the licensee's KYC duty. Practically, this matters less than it first appears, because of 1.2 below: the KYC framework's own text ties the connection to whoever is actually named as customer, not to whoever technically resells.[^1][^2][^3]

**1.2 The "list of end-users" clause is not a free pass for Model A.** The May 2024 expanded instructions require the business connection's KYC filing to include a list of end-users with name, designation, and identity-document details, and explicitly note this end-user list requirement is only optional where end-users are not identifiable (e.g., R&D/testing SIMs). A platform holding numbers in its own name and reselling to clients does not fit the "unidentifiable end-user" carve-out, since Calevate's clients are entirely identifiable at allocation time. Furnishing the client as "end-user" while the licensee's KYC records list the platform as customer-of-record likely satisfies the letter of the end-user list requirement, but it does **not** make the client the subscriber of record — the connection remains registered to the platform, which is the crux of where liability sits (see 1.4).[^2]

**1.3 Reselling telecom capacity requires DoT authorisation.** The clearest primary evidence is the **UL-VNO (Unified License – Virtual Network Operator)** category itself: DoT's own eServices portal lists UL-VNO application requirements including Certificate of Incorporation and a Board Resolution, confirming that virtual-network-operator resale of telecom capacity is a licensed activity requiring a company-level authorisation from DoT, not something an unlicensed SaaS can do on its own account. Model A as described — buying business connections and allocating them for a monthly fee — functions like unlicensed virtual-number resale unless the platform is itself the UL-VNO or is merely acting as an authorised reseller/distributor under an existing licensee's umbrella (a status the competitor's ₹649/month numbers plausibly rely on, i.e., they are likely reselling under an underlying UL-VNO's authorisation rather than holding their own license). This is CONFIRMED as to UL-VNO's own licensing requirement, but UNRESOLVED as to whether a SaaS platform can lawfully resell *without* itself being a UL-VNO, by riding on a licensed VNO's downstream reseller/agent programme — no primary source settles this distinction.[^4]

**1.4 Telecommunications Act 2023 s.3(7) risk in Model A is real but narrower than outright prohibition.** The June 2025 DoT directive on internet telephony KYC exists precisely because "some licensees were offering services under the Business Connection category without performing proper end-user verification, presuming that existing KYC norms were limited to traditional mobile services" — DoT explicitly closed this gap and gave licensees a 90-day compliance window ending September 14, 2025, after which non-compliance is a licence breach with penal exposure for the **licensee**. Section 3(7)-type fraud provisions are aimed at obtaining an identifier *on someone else's identity without their knowledge/authorisation* — in Model A, the platform is not impersonating the client, it is contracting as the named subscriber and allocating usage to a disclosed client. The more realistic exposure is not criminal fraud under s.3(7) but a **regulatory/licence-compliance failure by the underlying access provider or VNO** if end-user KYC is not properly captured, which cascades reputational and commercial risk (number suspension) onto the platform reselling the number. This is REPORTED analysis, not confirmed against a s.3(7) prosecution or DoT guidance directly addressing SaaS resellers.[^5][^6]

**1.5 Service-only restriction on transactional numbers is a DLT/numbering-series rule, not a mere contractual preference.** The 160-series (and 1600-series sub-allocation) was created by DoT specifically to segregate service/transactional voice traffic from promotional traffic; DoT's own May 2024 PIB release states the 160xxxxxxx series "shall exclusively be used for service/transactional voice calls" and that TSPs must verify entities before assignment and obtain an undertaking to that effect. TCCCPR itself defines Promotional, Transactional, and Service calls as distinct categories carrying different consent and registration requirements, and 140-series numbers are reserved exclusively for promotional/telemarketing calls while 160/1600-series is reserved for transactional/service calls — misuse triggers disconnection and financial penalties, now up to ₹10 lakh per instance in the BFSI context and graded penalty ladders (₹2 lakh/₹5 lakh/₹10 lakh) more generally under the 2025 TCCCPR amendments. This means Calevate's competitor's transactional/service-only restriction on ₹649/month numbers is a **regulatory constraint enforced through DLT header/number-series registration**, not a discretionary contract term — and it directly implies **Model A, as currently offered by the competitor, cannot lawfully support promotional/cold outbound calling** on those numbers without separate 140-series registration and a distinct consent framework.[^7][^8][^9][^10][^11][^12]

**1.6 The competitor's exposure.** Because 160-series/transactional numbers are explicitly restricted to BFSI-regulated entities under the 2025-26 TRAI directions in at least the 1601-prefix sub-series, and because normal 10-digit numbers used for "service" calls without proper DLT/PE registration risk being treated as Unregistered Telemarketer activity, a reseller issuing bulk business-connection numbers to arbitrary SMB clients under a blanket "transactional/service-only" label — without verifying each client's PE registration, content-template approval, and BFSI/non-BFSI status — carries genuine exposure to DoT/TRAI enforcement, borne first by the underlying licensee/VNO but contractually and reputationally by the reseller. This is a reasoned inference from primary sources rather than a documented enforcement case against a specific named competitor, so it is marked REPORTED/inferred, not CONFIRMED.[^9][^11]

## Part 2 — Model B: client holds the connection

**2.1 Model B is structurally cleaner but does not eliminate liability.** Having the client obtain their own business connection and register as PE on DLT under their own PAN/GST/CIN squarely satisfies the licensee KYC framework, since the subscriber of record and the PE are the same identifiable entity. This resolves the Part 1 ambiguities about reseller KYC and number-series misuse, because the client, not Calevate, is the number's registered service/transactional or promotional user. However, TCCCPR liability does not fully transfer — see 2.4.[^1][^2]

**2.2 Third-party API operation on client-owned accounts.** Sub-account and API-delegation models are standard commercial features, not exceptions requiring special telecom authorisation: Plivo explicitly documents sub-account structures where numbers are "billed and owned at the main-account level" but usable via delegated credentials for masking/session purposes, and voice-platform integration guides (e.g., Sarvam's Exotel connector) show that vendors routinely operate on a client's Exotel account using API credentials issued by the client's own account settings. This confirms the technical/contractual mechanism exists and is standard industry practice; no clause found in these sources restricts a client from sharing its own API credentials with a vendor, since the credentials remain within the client's own account, billed to the client. This is CONFIRMED for Plivo's account-and-sub-account architecture and REPORTED (by direct documentation, not a legal opinion) for whether this satisfies KYC "subscriber of record" requirements.[^13][^14][^15][^16]

**2.3 Onboarding friction in Model B is real and non-trivial.** Based on the KYC and DLT frameworks above, a client onboarding for Model B realistically must: (a) obtain a business connection/virtual number in its own name with CIN/GST/address KYC at the TSP; (b) register as Principal Entity on the DLT platform (fee and timeline not independently re-verified this pass, though TRAI's own guidance lists PE registration as the first of several required steps before any commercial communication); (c) register a header and content template appropriate to the call category (promotional/140-series vs transactional/160-series); (d) issue API credentials to the vendor. Industry examples of comparable 160-series provisioning cite roughly 7–14 business days end-to-end once documents are ready  — a similar order-of-magnitude timeline is a reasonable expectation for Model B generally, though this specific figure is documented for BFSI 160-series provisioning rather than general PE/DLT onboarding, so it should be treated as an illustrative REPORTED benchmark, not a guaranteed timeline for every client.[^17][^11][^2][^7][^9][^1]

**2.4 TM liability does not fully move off Calevate.** This is the most important corrective to the brief's working assumption. Primary TCCCPR-based commentary is explicit that liability for TCCCPR violations is **joint** between the Principal Entity and the registered Telemarketer: one source states plainly "Who Is Liable: Principal Entity and the registered telemarketer jointly," with penalties applied per call/instance. TRAI's own 2025 amendments impose graded financial deposits (₹2 lakh/₹5 lakh/₹10 lakh per successive violation) and mandate that access providers can suspend telecom resources of a sender found in violation, with deposits applied separately for registered and unregistered senders — but nothing in these amendments exempts a registered TM from consequences of a PE's non-compliant list, since the TM's DLT registration itself is a precondition for being permitted to originate the calls at all. Consequently, if a client (as PE) supplies a contact list obtained without valid consent and Calevate's platform dials it as the registered TM, exposure is realistically **joint**, not solely the PE's — the TM's telecom-resource access can be suspended and the TM can face its own DLT/TRAI penalties for facilitating non-compliant communication, regardless of what the underlying commercial contract with the client says about indemnity. This directly contradicts any assumption that Model B fully externalises consent risk.[^18][^10][^12]

**2.5 Termination consequences differ sharply.** In Model B, if a client leaves, Calevate loses only the API integration and campaign configuration — the number, its KYC, and its DLT registration remain the client's asset and liability, with no leftover connection to manage. In Model A, if a client leaves and the platform wants to reassign the same physical number to a new client, the underlying KYC instructions (tying the connection's KYC and end-user list to specific identified users) strongly imply a **fresh KYC filing is required for the new end-user**, and industry practice around number reassignment (particularly for masking/virtual numbers) typically includes a cooldown/quarantine period before reuse to avoid caller-ID confusion and residual consent-history contamination — this quarantine practice is common telephony-industry convention rather than a specifically documented DoT/TRAI mandate found in this research pass, so it is REPORTED, not CONFIRMED, though the underlying KYC-refresh requirement for a new identified end-user is directly supported by the "list of end-users" documentation itself.[^2]

## Part 3 — Concurrency and technical reality

**3.1–3.2 Concurrency is an account/channel property, not a number property.** Plivo's own documentation is unambiguous: "A single virtual number can host several thousand sessions and interactions… hosting several sessions on a virtual number does not impact the quality of the phone calls, as the phone number is only an identifier. The actual call media flows through dedicated calling channels for each parallel interaction". The purchasable unit for concurrency is therefore **calling channels/trunk capacity at the account level**, not the number itself; Plivo even publishes a formula for virtual-number pool sizing (2× concurrent sessions − 1) purely to avoid caller-ID collision across simultaneous sessions using the same first-party number, confirming numbers are an addressing/identifier resource while concurrency is sold as account-level channel capacity. This is CONFIRMED directly from Plivo's documentation and is a reasonable general inference for Exotel's architecture as well, though Exotel's specific channel-pricing terms were not independently verified in this pass.[^16]

**3.3 BYO-trunk/SIP-connect support.** This report did not independently verify Bolna's specific "connect" documentation for Exotel/Plivo/Vobiz within this research pass; the brief's own description that Bolna publishes connect guides for these three providers is plausible given standard SIP-trunk integration patterns in the industry but should be confirmed directly against Bolna's current docs before being relied upon, and is marked UNRESOLVED here pending that direct verification.

## Part 4 — 140 vs 160 series and AI-specific restrictions

**4.1–4.2 Number classes are strictly segregated by call category, both requiring DLT registration.** The 140-series is exclusively for promotional/telemarketing calls; the 160-series (with 1601 reserved for RBI/SEBI/PFRDA/IRDAI-regulated BFSI entities specifically) is exclusively for transactional/service calls, and both require pre-registered headers and content templates on DLT — using an unregistered mobile number for service/transactional calls is now itself a compliance violation exposing the sender to Unregistered Telemarketer penalties. Only Model B, where the client is a properly registered PE with header/template approval on the correct series, can lawfully run promotional/cold outbound; Model A's transactional/service-only number pool structurally cannot support promotional calling without a parallel 140-series registration process per client, which undermines the "buy in bulk, allocate" efficiency that makes Model A attractive.[^11][^7][^9]

**4.3 No AI-specific number-class restriction found, but AI does not escape TCCCPR.** One industry compliance source states plainly that "TRAI regulations for commercial calling apply equally to Voice AI systems and human agents. The TCCCPR framework makes no distinction based on who — or what — places the call," and separately notes telemarketers must disclose use of Auto Dialer or Robo-Call technology as senders of telemarketing messages. No primary DoT/TRAI source found imposes an AI-specific ban on any number class; the operative rule is simply that AI-originated calls are treated identically to human-agent calls for consent, category, and number-series purposes. This is REPORTED (based on secondary compliance-advisory commentary) rather than a specific TRAI regulation text directly naming AI systems, and should be confirmed with primary TCCCPR text if precision on this exact point is needed.[^10][^18]

## Part 5 — SMS and WhatsApp

**5.1 SMS under client ownership.** If the client is the registered PE, they can use their own registered header and content templates for SMS under the same DLT framework described above, keeping DLT content liability with them as sender of record — consistent with the joint-liability structure noted in 2.4, since Calevate would still bear TM-side obligations if it operates as the registered telemarketer transmitting on the client's behalf.[^17][^18]

**5.2 WhatsApp is a Meta-governed regime layered on top of, not replacing, Indian telecom law.** WhatsApp Business API access requires the business itself to be legally registered, with India-specific document requirements (GST certificate, Company Incorporation Certificate, or Shop Establishment Licence) matched exactly to the business name applying for verification. The WABA (WhatsApp Business Account) is the Meta-side container holding phone numbers, templates, and business profile; a BSP (Business Solution Provider) is a Meta-authorised reseller providing API access, and ownership of the WABA itself is retained by the business — one BSP's own documentation confirms "your business retains ownership of the WABA itself — you can remove [BSP]'s agency access at any time". This means for Calevate, the **client should own the WABA**, with Calevate integrated as a technical agency/BSP-partner with revocable access, mirroring the joint-liability logic of Model B for voice. WhatsApp messaging sits under Meta's own commerce/business policy framework primarily, though Indian consumer-protection and (once substantive provisions commence) DPDP obligations still attach to any personal data processed through it — WhatsApp is not itself brought under DLT/TCCCPR since that framework governs telecom-carrier voice/SMS, not OTT messaging.[^19][^20]

**5.3 Channel separation is real but number reuse across channels is technically possible with caveats.** WhatsApp Business API requires a phone number not currently active on the consumer WhatsApp app, and can be a mobile or landline number capable of receiving an OTP via call for verification  — meaning the same number used for voice/SMS with a telecom carrier can, in principle, also be registered for WhatsApp Business, but doing so effectively "converts" that number's WhatsApp presence and requires careful sequencing (voice/SMS carrier registration and WhatsApp Business verification are independent processes that do not automatically stay in sync). This was not exhaustively verified against Exotel/Plivo-specific number-reuse constraints and should be treated as a practical planning consideration rather than a fully settled technical rule.[^19]

**5.4 Liability that stays with Calevate even on client-owned identity.** Even where SMS/WhatsApp/voice are built entirely on the client's registered identity, Calevate as the software/platform sending or triggering communications on the client's behalf functions as an agent or TM-equivalent, and the TCCCPR joint-liability structure discussed in 2.4 means Calevate retains exposure for facilitating non-compliant communication, DLT deregistration/suspension risk to its own TM registration, and potential contractual liability under whatever DPA/MSA terms are signed — building on client identity reduces but does not eliminate Calevate's regulatory footprint.

## Part 6 — Recommendation

| Dimension | Model A (platform-owned) | Model B (client-owned) |
|---|---|---|
| Legality/confidence | UNRESOLVED/adverse — resale without own UL-VNO licence is questionable [^4]; transactional-number promotional-use restriction blocks cold outbound [^7][^9][^11] | CONFIRMED cleaner — subscriber-of-record and PE align, satisfying core KYC intent [^1][^2] |
| Platform's liability | Higher — bears licensee-adjacent KYC exposure plus TM-side joint liability if TM-registered | Lower but not zero — joint TCCCPR liability persists as TM [^18][^10][^12] |
| Client onboarding friction | Low (attractive for self-serve) | Higher — PE registration, header/template approval, ~1-2 weeks by analogy [^11] |
| Margin | Higher (bundled number resale revenue) | Lower (no number resale revenue) |
| Scalability | Limited by licensing/number-class constraints for promotional use | Scales cleanly for service/transactional; promotional requires per-client 140-series work either way |
| Exit/termination risk | Number reassignment likely needs fresh KYC/quarantine [^2] | Clean — client keeps their own number and DLT registration |

**6.2 Hybrid viability.** A hybrid — Model B for any client wanting promotional/cold outbound or with meaningful transaction volume, Model A only for a narrow inbound-only, transactional-only, low-risk self-serve tier — is more defensible than running Model A broadly, but Model A's core legal uncertainty (unlicensed resale, per 1.3) means even a narrow self-serve tier carries real risk for a solo founder with no corporate veil; it is not "risk-free at small scale," merely lower-volume risk.

**6.3 Entity choice implications.** Nothing in the sources found makes either model *require* a Private Limited Company as a matter of law — DLT/PE and TM registration accept proprietorships with a GST certificate or Shop & Establishment licence as proof-of-entity per prior research. However, Model A's unlicensed-resale exposure (1.3) is a corporate-liability question where the absence of a limited-liability shield (i.e., remaining a proprietorship) means any DoT/TRAI enforcement action or licence-breach cascade falls on the founder personally rather than a company — this is a strong practical argument for incorporating before running Model A at any scale, even though it is not a strict legal precondition either model.

**6.4 Stop-list.** Do not resell numbers under a "transactional/service-only" label while allowing or enabling any client to run promotional/cold outbound on them. Do not treat Model B as fully liability-transferring — retain DND scrubbing, consent-record expiry tracking, and audit logging as the platform's own operational discipline regardless of who is PE. Do not reassign a previously-KYC'd number to a new client without a fresh KYC filing. Do not build WhatsApp on a WABA the client cannot independently verify or reclaim ownership of.[^7][^9][^18][^11]

**6.5 Unresolved items requiring a telecom lawyer.** Whether a SaaS platform can lawfully resell business connections without holding its own UL-VNO licence, by operating strictly as an authorised downstream reseller/agent of a licensed VNO, is not settled by any primary source found and is the single most consequential open question for Model A. The exact quantum and mechanics of "quarantine" periods before number reassignment are not documented in a primary DoT/TRAI source. Bolna's specific BYO-trunk/SIP-connect technical requirements for Exotel/Plivo/Vobiz were not independently verified against current documentation. Whether Telecommunications Act 2023 s.3(7) has any enforcement history or specific DoT guidance addressing SaaS resellers (as opposed to end-user fraud) was not found and should be raised directly with counsel.

## Questions for a telecom lawyer

- Does furnishing a client's identity as "end-user" on a platform-held business connection discharge the licensee's KYC obligation, or does the licensing framework require the client itself to be the named subscriber for any commercially resold number?
- Can Calevate operate Model A lawfully as a reseller/agent under an existing UL-VNO's authorisation without holding its own VNO licence, and if so what documentation or agreement structure is required?
- Does Telecommunications Act 2023 s.3(7) create any exposure for a reseller allocating identified end-users to numbers held in the reseller's own name, or is it confined to concealment/impersonation fraud?
- What is the actual mandatory quarantine or re-KYC period, if any, before a business-connection number can be reassigned to a new end-user after a client departs?
