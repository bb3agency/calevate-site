## 1. Shape: a `control_plane` engine

ThinnestAI hosts the agent and the call. We configure it through their REST API (`https://app.thinnest.ai/api/v1`, `Bearer ta_live_…`, `api-reference/introduction.md`, `authentication.md`). Results come back as signed webhooks and are read from their call endpoints.

That is `AgentHosting = "control_plane"`: the shape the rented engine had before D-639. The generic machinery for it already exists:
- **the engine webhook route:** `voice-runtime /engine/{engine}`, with `engine_intake.py`, `webhook_routes.py` and `WEBHOOK_AUTH_BY_ENGINE`;
- **execution reconciliation:** `get_execution` / `list_executions`;
- **the rest:** the post-call pipeline over `ExecutionSnapshot`, and `kb_index_sync`.

**Reuse those seams; do not build parallel ones.**

| Calevate leg | ThinnestAI |
|---|---|

## 3. Inbound path, results and reconciliation

1. **Webhook registration.** A webhook is registered per agent at publish time, in the agent's own workspace (the header its handle names, §3b): `POST /webhooks` with `{agent, url: <WEBHOOK_BASE_URL>/engine/thinnest, events}`, the events being `call.analysed`, `call.completed`, `call.problem`, `contact.opted_out`, `conversation.escalated` and `lead.captured` (D-691; `call.problem` D-736). An endpoint registered with fewer is brought up to date in place with `PATCH /webhooks/{id} {events}`, keeping its secret.
   - The `signingSecret` is returned **once**. Store it sealed (envelope-encrypted like other credentials), keyed by agent.
   - Re-publishing must not create duplicates.
2. **voice-runtime `/engine/thinnest`.**
   - Verify `x-thinnest-signature-v2` against the agent's secret, chosen by the `agent` query parameter of our own url (the agent's handle, so the call is keyed in the agent's workspace, `voice-runtime/signed_intake.py`), then refuse (401) a `x-thinnest-delivered-at` more than 5 minutes from our clock. Never `sentAt`: every retry repeats the first attempt's bytes, so `sentAt` is hours old by design (`webhooks.md:110-131`).
   - Key the call: `call.completed` carries `id` only for a call placed through the API and `callRef` on every call, so the call is `id`, else `callRef` (an inbound completion used to be ignored as `webhook_unkeyable`); `call.analysed` is the Get Call object; `call.problem` and `contact.opted_out` name `callId`. The notices stay keyed on their event id.
   - Dedupe in the inbox on the signed event `id` (equal to `x-thinnest-event-id`, the same on every retry and re-send); the unit is `<event>:<event id>`. `x-thinnest-attempt` is recorded on `webhook_deliveries.attempts`; a duplicate logs it.
   - Route by event: call events to `ingest_engine_event`; `contact.opted_out` to `ingest_engine_opt_out` (§6); `lead.captured` and `conversation.escalated` to `ingest_engine_notice`, which normalises them with `parse_notice` (D-690), attributes them to the route's tenant and files them on the call their `data.callId` names (`call_engine_signals`, kind and time; null off a call; D-736); `call.problem` to `ingest_engine_problem` (step 4). The endpoint's own `test` delivery is acked and ignored.
   - Ack in under 500 ms (hard rule 3) and hand off to ARQ.
3. **Worker.**
   - **`call.completed` is a hint, never the call's content (D-848).** It is sent "the moment it ends" with how the call ended and how long it lasted; the transcript, summary and recording link come with `call.analysed`, "the same shape as Get Call" (`snapshots/2026-10-19/pages/api-reference/webhooks/create-webhook.md:281-285`). A delivery stands as the call's record only when it is `content_final` (`workers/engine_delivery.py`: analysed, and an answered call with talk time carries turns); otherwise the post-call job reads Get Call, and the hint stands only when Get Call answers 404. An answered call whose record still has no turns waits on the job's retry ladder (30 s, 120 s) and the last try settles it with alarm `engine_call_answered_without_transcript` and summary state `failed`, never "nothing was said". The post-call job id is the call and keeps no result (`settings.RESULT_WINDOW_S`), so a later delivery or a re-drive runs again once a run has finished; a stored reading made before the turns arrived is not reused; the outcome reads a `completed` row as answered; the poller re-drives an answered call that holds no turn when Get Call now has them. Calls settled empty before D-848: `python -m scripts.redrive_empty_calls --hours N [--apply]` in the workers container.
   - Upsert the call row: `engine_call_id` = their `id`, our `reference` comes back.
   - Write the transcript (redaction as today), run our own extraction pass, and update CRM and leads.
   - Meter the minutes (§5).
   - Copy `recording.url` to R2 (90 days) once `ready`; it expires on their side after 30 or 75 days.
   - Fire our client webhooks, including `call.recording_ready`.
4. **Live-call problems and call quality (D-736).** `call.problem` (`kind` `no_answer_from_model`, `byok_failed`, `slow_reply`, `voice_failed`; at most once per kind per call; held to about 20 a minute per agent) is filed once per kind per call in `call_engine_signals`, our kinds being `no_answer_from_model`, `own_key_failed`, `slow_reply`, `voice_failed`; the vendor's `detail` sentence is never stored. An `own_key_failed` on any agent alarms `engine_call_own_key_failed`: there is no backup on our own keys, so the caller heard ThinnestAI's apology (our model key) or nothing (our voice key), and the remedy is the key or its provider account (`snapshots/2026-10-13/pages/api-reference/bring-your-own-keys.md:171-178`; ThinnestAI's own "notification naming the provider" is in-app only, no webhook, `webhooks.md:77-86`). A Studio agent's `voice_failed` alarms `studio_call_voice_failed` (no fallback, §4a), a Clear agent's `voice_failed` `engine_call_voice_failed`, a failed answer of the platform's own model `engine_call_model_unanswered`; `slow_reply` alarms nothing. `quality` (`turns`, `responseMs`, `firstTokenMs` median/p90/max, `longSilences`, `longestSilenceSeconds`, `interruptions`, `modelErrors`, `rescues`, any null) is read by `parse_quality` from `call.completed` (may be null), `call.analysed` and Get Call into `calls.engine_quality`, never overwritten by a null. Both show as "How the call went" on the admin view of a call (`CallQualityCard`; the words name no provider). Across every client, the admin **Voice response time** screen (D-770, `/admin/ops/reply-times`) aggregates `engine_quality` per call (median of the calls' median `responseMs`, 90th percentile of their p90, the `firstTokenMs` share, model errors, rescues, interruptions, long silences) with the `slow_reply` problems, by client, agent, Clear/Studio rung and IST day, against the console targets `reply_time_typical_target_ms` (1,600 ms) and `reply_time_slow_target_ms` (2,200 ms, judged on each call's p90; D-896), and split by the voice settings each call ran on (`calls.voice_settings`). The healer counts a call as slow when its p90 reply passes 2.5 s or it raised `slow_reply`.
5. **Reconciliation sweep (required).** ThinnestAI retries a failed attempt after 1m, 5m, 30m, 2h and 6h and switches an endpoint off after five events in a row that failed every attempt (`snapshots/2026-10-08/pages/api-reference/webhooks.md:110-155`). So:
   - list calls since the last watermark and settle any we have not;
   - check each endpoint's `enabled` flag, `PATCH enabled:true` with an alarm when it was switched off, then `POST /webhooks/{id}/redeliver {since}` — the whole window the vendor keeps, 7 days less a 30-minute margin (`snapshots/2026-10-08/pages/api-reference/webhooks/redeliver-webhook-events.md:7,390-397`; `reliability/engine_webhooks.redelivery_since`). An endpoint still enabled but reporting `failuresInARow > 0` is asked to re-send too. Both the publish path and the sweep do this (`ensure_agent_webhook`, D-693). Re-sent events keep their ids, so the inbox absorbs any already handled.

- **Buying** (client `/v1/numbers/own/*`, admin `/v1/admin/engine-workspaces/tenants/{id}/numbers/*`). `purchase_readiness` decides for both: account open, workspace active, KYC verified, business details `accepted` with `canRent`, and an attested client price (OPERATIONS §2 gate 26, ₹499). The vendor's `monthlyPrice` is OUR cost and is never shown as the client's price (hard rule 7). The client picks a city (`list-available-cities.md`), searches (`GET /phone-numbers/available`, at most 20 a page, `nextCursor` back as `cursor`, `search-available-phone-numbers.md:7`), confirms our price and buys. The order: every refusal that costs nothing, the client wallet check, then `POST /phone-numbers` — which charges the first month to our balance at once and refuses "before anything is bought or charged" (`rent-phone-number.md:7`) — then, in one transaction, the number recorded through `record_engine_number` and the client's first month collected by the same rental path every number uses (`billing/number_rental.collect_number_rental`, D-665/D-691), then `PATCH {agent, callingAgent}`. A vendor refusal charges the client nothing; a 402 (our balance) alarms `engine_number_rent_unfunded`.
- **One number per click.** The request is committed in `engine_number_purchases` as `renting` under the caller's idempotency key before the vendor is asked, so a retry or double-click finds the same row: a finished one answers the same number, a refused one the same refusal, and an unfinished one is resolved by reading the number back from the workspace (`engine_number_purchase_unconfirmed` until it can be), never by renting again. The rent page documents no idempotency key (`rent-phone-number.md:343-344`); since 12 Oct 2026 our purchase id is sent as one anyway (`calevate-rent-<purchase id>`), and the read-back stays the guarantee until ThinnestAI says the route honours it (gate T-19).
- **Moving, not re-renting (D-737).** A number recorded for a client but held in another workspace (the platform account, or one the client no longer uses) moves into the client's own workspace with its digits: `POST /phone-numbers/{n}/move {toWorkspace}`, sent as the workspace that holds it (api-reference/phone-numbers/move-phone-number, read 11 Oct 2026). It arrives answered by nobody and is re-attached from our row; a rented one is charged to the client from its next renewal (`price_recorded_number`). A repeat of a finished move answers the same for a day. `number_on_live_call`, `number_move_in_progress` and `number_move_unavailable` (503) queue `move_engine_number` every 2 minutes for 12 tries (`engine_number_move_failed` after); `number_move_needs_business_details` (an Indian number needs the target's approved details) and the rest are refused in words. Admin **Move to client workspace** on the Numbers page; never automatic, because a platform-held number is a test number until an operator says it is the client's. The shared trial number never moves.
- **Releasing.** `DELETE /phone-numbers/{n}?confirm=release`: permanent, the number goes back to the pool and this month's rent is not refunded (`release-phone-number.md:7`). Our rental stops with `released_at`, and the client is not refunded the current period either; both screens say so before the button. **Release our record** (admin, `…/numbers/{number_id}/forget`) only forgets our row: for a number the vendor no longer holds, or a platform-held test number taken back from a client; a number the client's workspace still holds is refused there, since releasing it is what stops the vendor's rent.
- **Held in the platform account.** A number rented in our developer workspace before D-693 (an unscoped handle) is shown as "held in the platform account", may be recorded against a client for testing only, and can answer only an agent still in the developer workspace (`engine_number_other_workspace`); an agent republished into its client's workspace leaves it behind, and buying a number for an agent not yet republished is refused (`engine_number_agent_not_moved`). Gate T-21.
- **Record this number.** For a number already in the client's workspace that we have not recorded, the client's Numbers page offers **Record this number** (`POST /v1/admin/numbers/tenants/{id}/engine/record`); a hand-typed `thinnest` number (`POST /v1/admin/tenants/{id}/numbers`) goes through the same function. Both refuse a number the workspace does not hold (`engine_number_not_held`), a typed handle that is not the vendor's (`engine_number_ref_mismatch`) and a number another client's agent answers (`engine_number_answered_by_other_client`). Handle, `engine_owned` (= `source: rented`) and series are read, never typed.
- **Price.** A rented number gets `client_inr_per_month` from the attested number price and its first period is collected in the same transaction (D-665); `renew_number_rentals` bills it from then on. One recorded before it was priced is priced from its NEXT renewal (`rental_charged_from`). A brought number is not priced. Our own rental cost is metered by `meter_number_rentals` at the operator-attested `thinnest_number_month` rent plus the attested wallet top-up fee (ThinnestAI charges it in rupees from our balance; D-840); until the rent is attested each such number alarms `number_rental_price_missing`.
- **Attachment; we are the master.** From our row: an agent that answers (`inbound`/`both`) is the number's `agent` with `callingAgent` null (an agent calls out on its own line, `calls/place-call.md:974`); an outbound-only agent is lent it as `callingAgent`; a released number or one on no live published agent has neither. Sent on record, on attach and by the daily sweep, only the fields that differ (re-sending one is a 409). `agent` is applied before `callingAgent` and a refusal keeps what was applied (`update-phone-number.md:7`): a partial or refused apply alarms `engine_number_attachment_failed`, and the sweep retries. A number and its agent must be in the same workspace.
- **Daily sweep** (`reconcile_engine_number_attachments`): the developer workspace and every active client workspace are listed once, and each of our numbers is matched in the workspace its handle names — `engine_number_unrecorded` (held, recorded by nobody; never adopted), `engine_number_missing_at_vendor`, `engine_number_attachment_repaired`. At most 100 numbers are re-read a run.
- **Our developer workspace's own application** is still shown read-only on the ops page; `engine_business_details_lapsed` carries `platform` for it.

### 3b. Workspaces: one customer workspace per client (D-693)

The founder's decision of 8 Oct 2026. Vendor paths are under `thinnest-findings/mirror/snapshots/2026-10-08/pages/`.

**The model.** A ThinnestAI *customer* is a workspace we create for one of our clients, with its own agents, contacts, knowledge and numbers; our one API key reaches it by adding `Thinnest-Workspace: <org_ id>` to any request, which then "runs inside that customer as if the customer had sent it", and everything it uses is billed to our balance (`api-reference/customers.md:9-11,68-91,185-189`). Every Calevate client gets one. What lives where:

| Developer workspace (ours) | Each client's customer workspace |
|---|---|
| Our account, plan and balance; `POST/GET/DELETE /customers` (only from here, `customers.md:102`) | The client's agents, their knowledge, actions and webhooks |
| Our Cartesia key with voice-only BYOK ON (D-727), where free-trial agents speak Studio; every published agent not on a Studio voice that would follow it, here or in a customer workspace with no key of its own (`api-reference/bring-your-own-keys.md:128-133`), is set `byok: off` first | The client's calls and recordings, and their `costMicro`; our Cartesia key with voice-only BYOK ON when the client uses Studio (D-717) |
| The platform voice catalogue and the admin's clones | The client's numbers, rented in its business name, and its business-details application |
| Objects made before D-693 (unscoped handles), until each moves; test numbers "held in the platform account" | The client's contacts and do-not-call list (D-691's person-level writes) |

**One resolver.** `tenancy/engine_workspace.resolve_workspace` (and `workspace_for_tenant` for a caller with no session) reads `tenant_engine_workspaces` and answers the tenant's `org_` id only while its status is `active`; in every other state it answers None, "not provisioned". Nothing falls back to the developer workspace for a client resource: a caller that gets None refuses (`engine_workspace_not_provisioned`, 409, "this account's voice workspace is not ready yet") or records that nothing was sent. `is_own_workspace` also refuses our developer workspace's id, which is `org_…` like every customer's (`api-reference/workspace/get-workspace.md:327-351`): it is read from `GET /workspace` by the provisioning job and can be set as `thinnest_developer_workspace_id` (gate T-16). `own_workspace`, which D-691's do-not-call push and contact erasure ask when queued and again when sent, delegates to it.

**One header.** `engine/thinnest_workspace.workspace_headers` is the only place the header is built; every ThinnestAI client in `apps/api/engine/` calls it. Three classes of route are enforced there, not at each call site:
- *developer-only* — customers, `GET /models`, `GET /workspace`: a header is refused before the request is built. The BYOK routes are NOT developer-only since D-717: they follow the ambient workspace ("Every request below also works for a customer, with the `Thinnest-Workspace` header", `bring-your-own-keys.md:137`), and switching OUR developer workspace's BYOK on is refused by the adapter (`refuse_developer_byok_on`);
- *tenant-only* — `POST /agents`, `POST /phone-numbers`, number search and cities, `PUT /phone-numbers/business-details`, `POST /do-not-call`, `GET /contacts`, `DELETE /contacts/{id}`: refused without a customer workspace, because in the developer workspace they would act for every legacy client at once;
- *everything else* follows the object: the workspace its handle names.

The workspace comes from the object's handle where there is one — `<raw>@<org_…>` (`calevate_shared/engine_scope.py`), on agent routes, calls, webhooks and numbers; an unscoped handle names an object in the developer workspace, every one of them made before D-693 — from the tenant's resolver for a new object, and from `in_workspace(...)` for a listing a sweep walks. A handle of one workspace used inside another is a programming error (`WorkspaceScopeError`). `tests/thinnest_workspace_header_guard_test.py` fails if a vendor call for a tenant resource omits the header.

**Provisioning** (`apps/workers/engine_workspaces.py`).
- `provision_engine_workspace` is queued through the outbox on the account's FIRST PAYMENT (D-697: `billing/first_payment.on_payment_credited`, in the transaction that credits it), not at creation; a free-trial account has none. It looks for a customer carrying our `externalId` (`calevate-<tenant uuid>`, `customers.md:55`) before creating one, creates with an `Idempotency-Key` (`:59-60`), reads back on a 409 for our `externalId`, and restores a deleted customer still held rather than making a second (`:155-159`). It refuses to record our developer workspace's id as a client's (`engine_workspace_is_developer`).
- States (`tenant_engine_workspaces.status`): `pending` → `active`; `plan_limit` when `POST /customers` answers 402 (`customers.md:62-66`; `plan_limit` / `plan_required`, `errors.md:156-157`), alarming `engine_workspace_plan_limit`; `failed` after any other failure, alarming `engine_workspace_provisioning_failed` with the error code on the admin page; `offboarding` and `deleted` on account closure. Until `active` the client cannot publish an agent or buy a number, and its screens say the account is being set up.
- Plan caps, a deleted customer counting until it is erased: Free and pay-as-you-go 3, Pro 100, Scale 1,000, Enterprise 10,000 (`customers.md:191-200`). `thinnest_customer_plan` records which plan we are on for the ops headroom and the alarm text; the vendor's 402 is what enforces it. On pay-as-you-go the fourth tenant is refused (gate T-8).
- `retry_engine_workspaces` (daily) queues every live tenant that has PAID and has no row (`first_payment.PAID_TENANT_SQL`, D-697), and every tenant still `pending`, `failed` or `plan_limit`, at most 50 a tick. An admin's **Retry provisioning** (`POST /v1/admin/engine-workspaces/tenants/{id}/provision`) queues the same job.
- On activation the business details are queued (§3a) and the tenant's existing do-not-call list is pushed to the new workspace through `push_engine_dnc`.

**Agents.** A new agent is created in its client's workspace (`agents/service._in_client_workspace`); a publish for a tenant whose workspace is not active is refused with `engine_workspace_not_provisioned`, and nothing is created in the developer workspace except a free-trial account's agents (§3c, D-697). The 8 Oct snapshot documents no way to move an agent between workspaces (nothing under `api-reference/agents/`), so an agent made before D-693 is recreated: its next publish recreates it in the client's workspace under a new vendor id (`_moves_workspace`). In the same transaction (`_after_workspace_move`) the claims on the old agent's knowledge copies are cleared so the D-689 catch-up attaches every live source to the new one, the webhook and the in-call actions are registered for the new handle, and outbox job `retire_moved_engine_agent` is queued. That job runs only after the publish commits, waits (retrying every 5 minutes) while `GET /calls?agent=&status=connected` shows a call on the old agent, then retires its actions and webhook, deletes it and deactivates the old route; if a call is still connected after the retries, or the delete fails, `engine_agent_retire_failed` alarms and the old agent is left for an operator. The old route stays active until then, so a late delivery for a call it took before the move still verifies. Deleting an agent on a live call is refused, `409 agent_on_live_call` (api-reference/errors, read 11 Oct 2026; gate T-17), which the job waits out like a connected call. A number in the developer workspace does not follow (§3a).

**Sweeps.** A vendor listing answers for one workspace, so every reconciliation reads each workspace in turn: the developer workspace first, then the active client workspaces, at most 100 a tick, resuming from a per-sweep Redis cursor (`workers/workspace_walk.py`; a lost cursor restarts the rotation and skips nothing).
- walked: executions (`pipeline.reconcile_executions`), charges (`workers/engine_charges.py`, `costMicro` per workspace; the balance is ours alone), and `sweep_engine_workspaces` (client workspaces only: business details, BYOK inheritance, pre-D-797 clone copies);
- all at once into one picture: KB orphans (`workers/kb_orphans.py`) and numbers (§3a);
- per agent route, following the handle's workspace with no walk: engine drift, KB drift, and the webhook sweep.

**Webhooks and actions.** Registered per agent in the agent's workspace (§3, D-691's v2 signature and event-id dedupe unchanged). The vendor's single `includeCustomers` endpoint (`api-reference/webhooks.md:48-65`) is not used: the receiver already resolves the tenant from our own agent route and the per-agent secret. Every action call carries `X-Workspace-Id` (`agent/custom-api.md:116-119`); `worker/engine_actions.py` compares it with the agent's workspace and refuses a mismatch with 401, alarming `engine_action_workspace_mismatch`.

**BYOK and Studio (D-717, 10 Oct 2026).** Customers use our keys and scope while OUR BYOK is on, unless one brings a complete set of its own, which then overrides ours; "A customer with its own complete set and its own switch on keeps using its own" (`bring-your-own-keys.md:129-133`). Since D-727 (11 Oct 2026) our developer workspace's switch is ON, voice only, so a free-trial agent there can speak Studio with `byok: "voice"` (named outright since 20 Oct 2026, never `workspace`, so no agent depends on a workspace default); an agent set `byok: "off"` never uses our keys (`:69-79`), the agent default is `workspace` (`:304-306`), so every agent states it and the adapter refuses one that does not (`engine_agent_byok_unstated`), and Studio ready sets every published non-Studio agent that would follow the switch `off` and reads it back before switching (`agents/studio_voices.ensure_developer_studio`). Our key is also switched on in the customer workspace of each paying client that uses Studio, at its first Studio publish (`agents/studio_voices.ensure_studio_workspace`: that client's published Clear agents set `byok: off` and read back, the key, `PATCH /byok {enabled: true, scope: "voice"}`, a read-back expecting `using: own`). The hourly `workers/studio_voice_key.sweep_studio_workspaces` verifies and repairs the developer workspace (`studio_developer_repaired`, `studio_developer_drift`) and each Studio client workspace. The docs say a customer "follows the same rule on its own agents" for per-agent `byok` (`bring-your-own-keys.md:79-80`): VENDOR-DOCS-STATED, untested live, gate T-15.

**Clones (D-797).** The admin clones in the developer workspace, and every such clone can be used by every customer workspace's agents, automatically: inside a customer it is listed by `GET /voices` with `sharedFromDeveloper: true`, a customer cannot rename or delete it, and it counts against our clone limit only (`snapshots/2026-10-15/pages/channels/voice-clone.md:114-126`; `api-reference/customers.md:236-241`; `api-reference/voices/list-voices.md:579-589`). So a client agent on one of our clones speaks the shared clone; nothing is copied. The shared clone keeps its id: a customer's `GET /voices` lists it under our clone's `voiceId` (ThinnestAI's email of 18 Oct 2026, D-815, VENDOR-STATED; the 18 Oct docs snapshot names only the `sharedFromDeveloper` mark). `agents/shared_clones.client_voice_id` still reads the client workspace's own `GET /voices` and accepts only a `sharedFromDeveloper` entry carrying our clone's id, because the clone is listed there only on Pro and above and not after we delete it; anything else (no shared entry, or no Studio voices below Pro, `list-voices.md:450-453`) refuses the publish as `shared_clone_not_listed` and logs `engine_shared_clone_not_listed` with the workspace and the count of shared entries. It never falls back to a copy or to the developer workspace. The drift sweep's settings read-back resolves the voice through the same door (`shared_clones.voice_in_workspace`), so an agent published on a pre-D-797 copy reads as voice drift once and is repaired onto the shared clone, and a clone the workspace stops listing is skipped, never "repaired" to an id it does not hold. Deleting our clone moves every agent on it, ours and every client's, to a standard voice (`voice-clone.md:124-126`); the delete route counts that and also deletes the legacy copies. Legacy: `engine_voice_clone_copies` and `platform_voice_catalog.sample_object_key` are no longer written (hard rule 8, dropped in a later release), and new clone recordings are no longer kept. Before D-797 each client workspace got its own copy made from the admin's sealed recording (D-693).

**Held numbers (D-860).** When a client stops (account closed, or prepaid wallet below zero for `number_hold_after_negative_days`), each number rented in its own workspace is held: detached with `PATCH {agent: null, callingAgent: null}` ("Disconnect" leaves it rented and charged, `snapshots/2026-10-19/pages/channels/phone-numbers.md:787-791`), charged nothing by our renewal job, and released 24 hours before the earliest instant its next rent could fall, at most 30 days after the hold began. The rent day comes from the number's `since` (`get-phone-number.md:702-707`, read by `ThinnestNumbers.get_number` as `rented_since`) and our `created_at`, each read in UTC and in IST: the platform charges "every month on the same day" (`rent-phone-number.md:449-451`) without naming the zone or the hour, so the earliest candidate wins. A top-up or a reopening before then reattaches it. `campaigns/number_holds.py`, `workers/number_holds.py`, `runbooks/number-holds.md`.

**Offboarding.** Closing an account queues `offboard_engine_workspace`, which does nothing while a number there is held (D-860) and is queued again by the last hold's release. Then: each number in the client's workspace released with `?confirm=release` and `released_at` set, each of its agents' actions retired and the agent deleted, then `DELETE /customers/{id}` — refused with 409 while it still holds a rented number, which is why the numbers go first (`customers.md:136-146`). Afterwards requests into it answer 410, its webhooks are off, and it is erased 30 days later; until then `POST /customers/{id}/restore` brings it back (`:148-161`). Restoring the Calevate account re-provisions, which restores the customer if the vendor still holds it. Released numbers do not come back. A failure alarms `engine_workspace_offboarding_failed`; an admin's **Offboard** (`POST /v1/admin/engine-workspaces/tenants/{id}/offboard`) re-queues it. Runbook: `runbooks/engine-workspace-provisioning.md`.

**Surfaces.** The client's admin Identity verification page shows the approval read-only (`kyc/NumberApprovalPanel.tsx`, with Check again now) The voice-workspace panel on the client's admin Numbers page (`admin/tenants/[tenantId]/numbers/WorkspacePanel.tsx`: status, workspace id, last error, business details with Send business details and Refresh status, numbers, Retry provisioning, Offboard); the ops summary `GET /v1/admin/engine-workspaces/summary` (workspaces provisioned against tenants, failures, plan headroom); the client's Numbers page as three steps — verify your business, business details, buy a number — naming no provider (D-679).

### 3c. Free-trial test calls (D-697)

The founder's decision of 9 Oct 2026: a free trial is for experiencing Calevate. Vendor paths are under `thinnest-findings/mirror/snapshots/2026-10-08/pages/api-reference/`.

**Who is a trial account.** One whose newest trial has not converted and which has not yet made its first payment (`compliance/trial_access.restricting_trial`; "paid" is `organizations.first_paid_at`, `billing/first_payment.PAID_TENANT_SQL`). Every new account is one from birth, signed up or invited (D-853). It may create and edit its agents and place outbound test calls from any READY agent, live or published and not taking calls, including one that only answers calls (D-853; `check_dispatch(trial_call=True)`); it is refused inbound answering (an answer-only agent cannot be published to take calls, `trial_inbound_unavailable`), campaigns (`trial_campaigns_unavailable`), buying or recording numbers (`trial_numbers_unavailable`), KYC writes (`trial_kyc_unavailable`) and every other outbound call (`trial_live_outbound_unavailable`). Each refusal is asked in the gate it belongs to (`check_dispatch`, `launch_blockers`, `purchase_readiness` and `agents.service.provision_number`, the KYC write dependency, `publish_agent`).

**Its agents live in the developer workspace.** `publish_agent` makes a trial account's agent there (`engine/thinnest_workspace.trial_agent_in_developer_workspace` admits that one `POST /agents`); once the account has paid and its own workspace is active, the move job recreates it there through D-693's publish path (D-859, below). Closing the account retires its developer-workspace agents (`agents.service.retire_developer_workspace_agents`, the `retire_moved_engine_agent` job); a tenant erasure lists them among the vendor deletions it owes, as it does every engine agent.

**One shared number.** `from` must be one of the agent's numbers, "its own, or one lent to it for calling" (`calls/place-call.md:430-433,971-977`), and a number is answered by at most one agent and lent to at most one (`phone-numbers/update-phone-number.md:144-145`). So each test call:
1. passes `check_dispatch(trial_call=True)`: the halt, the drain, the account, the agent and its truthful answers, the trial's own limits (ended, free minutes, today's cap), the pledge, the agreements, the spend cap, calling hours, India only, the do-not-call list and consent; not KYC or the carrier application (the caller ID is ours, gate T-25); no call asks an autodialer notice since D-762;
2. holds the platform's ONE trial line: an advisory lock serialises a count of live `calls.trial_call` rows (`trial_lines_in_use()`, migration `a2f7c4e9d61b`) with the intent row, and from commit the row's own status is the hold, until the call ends or ages out at `trial_call_max_seconds` + 3 minutes. A second trial account meets "Another test call is in progress. Try again in a minute." (`trial_line_busy`). What re-pointing `callingAgent` does to a live call is UNKNOWN (gate T-23), so the line is held for the whole call;
3. lends the number: `PATCH /phone-numbers/{number}` with `agent: null` and `callingAgent: <the trial agent>` (`update-phone-number.md:451-470`; "where the number answers is unchanged" by `callingAgent`, and `agent: null` "makes nothing answer it"), through `campaigns/engine_numbers.lend_trial_line`. The borrower is then given `voice.surfaces: ["phone"]` (lending does not add it) and its answer must also show `voice.answersCalls: true`: Place Call needs Answer calls on, `phone` in surfaces and a dialable number, own or lent; a lent number is outbound only and lent to one agent at a time (ThinnestAI's email of 18 Oct 2026, D-815). A publish always sends Answer calls on, so off is our own pause or a console edit; it is reported (`engine_agent_not_answering`), never switched on. A refusal closes the dial unplaced and alarms `trial_line_lend_failed`;
4. places the call: `POST /calls` with `from` = the shared number, `to` = the typed number and `overrides.maxCallSeconds` = the console limit or what is left of the free minutes, never under the vendor's 60 (`calls/place-call.md:1103-1126`).

**Choosing the shared number.** The ops console panel **Shared trial number** (`GET /v1/ops/trial-number`) offers only numbers our developer workspace holds (`GET /phone-numbers` with no workspace header) that no client has recorded; `ops.config_service.set_value` re-checks every write of `trial_caller_number` against the same list (`ops/trial_number.assert_trial_number_selectable`). An operator smoke-tests the whole path with `POST /v1/admin/tenants/{id}/trial/test-call` (`runbooks/trial-calling.md`).

**No AI answers the shared number (D-742).** Inbound to it never reaches a trial client's agent. Its line is one agent in the developer workspace named "Calevate shared trial number (no answer)", never recorded in `agents` and no client's, with `voice.answersCalls: false` and `voice.unavailableMessage` = `engine_numbers.TRIAL_LINE_MESSAGE` ("Thanks for calling back. This number only places test calls for businesses trying Calevate, so no one can take your call here. Goodbye."). English, because the phone provider's own Indian-English voice reads it and plays the standard sentence for Telugu script; no greeting (D-708); ≤300 characters (api-reference/agents/create-agent `AgentVoiceInput`; channels/voice "When Answer calls is off", read 11 Oct 2026). `converge_trial_line` finds it by name, makes it once, repairs it when found answering or reworded, and makes it the number's line (switching its voice on only for the attach if the platform refuses a paused agent); each lend then sends `callingAgent` only. It runs from the daily number sweep, on the first test call of a process, and from the ops panel's **Set it up now** (`POST /v1/ops/trial-number/line`), which shows its state. Whether the message is billed is not documented (UNKNOWN; if it is, one 30-second Premium pulse, about ₹1.38). Spoken on ThinnestAI's Plivo and Twilio numbers only. The number is never recorded against a client (`engine_number_is_trial_number`), the daily number sweep leaves it out of `engine_number_unrecorded` and alarms `trial_number_recorded_to_client` if it finds a record.

**Sync.** A test call is a `calls` row with `trial_call = true`, written by `dispatch_call` and completed by the normal webhook and poller, so its transcript, recording and minutes show up like any call. The trial's free minutes count those rows' `duration_s`; the daily cap counts them since IST midnight. A trial writes nothing to the ledger (D-536 unchanged); its minutes are metered at our cost.

**The first payment** (`billing/first_payment.on_payment_credited`, the one hook every payment-credit path calls in its crediting transaction: the gateway top-up, an operator's bank-transfer credit and its restatement, which passes the restated total so a transfer first recorded under the minimum counts once stated correctly, D-859) at or above `payment_routes.MIN_TOPUP_INR` stamps `first_paid_at` once, converts a running trial (or keeps the data of one that ended unsold), queues `provision_engine_workspace` and owes the owner the go-live guide (`go_live_guides`). ### 3d. The client's own actions (D-700)

A client's agent acts in the client's own systems during a call. Vendor paths are under `thinnest-findings/mirror/snapshots/2026-10-08/pages/`; the other vendors' facts are cited in `apps/api/actions/*.py` (read 9 Oct 2026).

**What exists.** Seven kinds (`actions/models.ACTION_KINDS`): `caller_lookup` (the caller's record from Zoho CRM, HubSpot, a shared Google Sheet or the client's API, matched on the caller's number), `calendar` (Google Calendar free times and booking, India time), `whatsapp` (a template from the client's own WhatsApp Business account via Meta Cloud API, AiSensy or Interakt), `payment_link` (a Razorpay Payment Link on the client's own account, sent on their WhatsApp), `crm` (create or update the caller's Zoho lead/contact or HubSpot contact), `sheets` (one row per call in a shared sheet, or a lookup), `custom_api` (the client's https endpoint). Each is set up on the agent's Actions; the accounts are connected on Integrations → Connected accounts.

**Why our endpoint and not the vendor's tools.** ThinnestAI has its own Cal.com, Grist, WhatsApp-template and helpdesk tools (`agent/actions.md:37-76`), configured in its console against accounts connected there. Ours keep the credentials sealed by us, run our dispatch gate (DNC) and messaging-consent ledger before anything reaches a caller, and audit every run. So each client action is a ThinnestAI **custom action** pointing at `POST /v1/worker/engine-actions/{engine}/client/{name}?agent=<handle>`, exactly like our four platform tools (§8).

**Registered and kept in step** (`reliability/engine_actions.py`). An action is live when the agent's master switch and the action are both on (`actions.service.in_call_tools`). Every live action is a vendor action on the agent, in the agent's own workspace (D-693, the handle carries it): name = the action's name (`^[a-z][a-z0-9_]{2,39}$`, `api-reference/actions/create-action.md`), description = the client's text plus a platform sentence binding the model to the answer's `say`, parameters = the values the model fills (static and call values are applied by us), `speakBefore` = the line the agent says while it works (≤ 200 characters), the per-agent sealed `X-Agent-Secret`, and a body carrying `{{call.id}}`. The converge creates, repairs, re-enables and DELETES — a vendor action of ours that is no longer live goes — at publish, in the drift sweep, and at once after any change on the Actions or Connections screens (`sync_client_actions_now`, which never fails the client's save: the in-call door refuses anything not live in our database). Unpublishing removes them all. At most four per agent (`THINNEST_CLIENT_ACTIONS_MAX`, refused as `client_actions_over_limit`; the vendor's "eight tools per agent", `agent/actions.md:213`, gate A-6).

**One call.** The route checks the agent's secret, the workspace header, the engine and the live call (`GET /calls/{id}` on that agent, ringing or connected); the vendor's own test, which has no call, runs nothing. It makes sure our `calls` row exists, then `actions/in_call.run_in_call_action` runs the action within 7 seconds (ThinnestAI asks for an answer within 10, `mcp/own-database.md:48`; gate A-8) or, for a CRM record or a sheet row, acknowledges at once and queues `run_client_action`. The answer is `{status, say, …}` like our own tools'. The caller's number is the vendor's `phone` on the call. Per call: two WhatsApp messages and two payment links per action, on top of ThinnestAI's thirty custom-action calls per conversation per hour (`agent/custom-api.md:147-155`).

**Knowing the caller.** OUTBOUND: before the dial, `agents.service.dispatch_call` runs the agent's live caller lookup (`actions/pre_dial.py`, 3 s, never a gate) and sends what it found as the call's `name` (≤ 120) and the variables `caller_name` and `caller_details` (≤ 150 each; `api-reference/calls/place-call.md:895-899, :912-917`), so there is no wait on the line. Every dial also sends `phone` (the number dialled, ten digits for +91), `call_details` (the contact's list row as `Header: value; ...`), `call_reason` (a call-back's or follow-up's redacted reason) and each list column as its own variable, which the THIS CALL lines `Number`, `Details` and `Why this call` read on an outbound or both-ways agent; list columns give way first at 20 variables (D-905, `engine/thinnest._dial_variables`). INBOUND: the live docs (read 10 Oct 2026, past the 8 Oct mirror) add a pre-answer hook, `voice.callStartUrl`. Every published agent's hook is OUR endpoint, `POST /v1/worker/engine-lookups/thinnest?agent=<ref>` (D-716, `reliability/engine_lookups.py`, `worker/engine_lookups.py`): the vendor mints the signing secret and returns it once on the PATCH that sets the address, we seal it on the agent's route (`call_start_secret_*`, `PLATFORM_KEK`) and rotate with `callStartSecret: "rotate"` when we hold none; each request is verified with the v2 webhook signature and answered within 0.8 s with up to 20 short variables from our CRM for that tenant (`crm/caller_lookup.py`: name, interest, caller known, call back allowed; last call topic, open call back and language only when the agent remembers callers), or `{"variables": {}}`. Phone and WhatsApp inbound only. The drift sweep reads `callStartUrl` back and alarms `engine_caller_lookup_repaired`. A client's own caller-lookup ACTION, where one is set up, still runs as the agent's first action with a 4 s budget; the agent's past-conversation memory (`pastConversations`) recognises a repeat caller with no delay. Whether an inbound agent reads a contact's name is UNKNOWN (gate A-11).

**Agent-to-agent hand-off** exists on ThinnestAI (`handOver.agents`, channels/voice "Handing a call on", read 11 Oct 2026) and is not offered (gate A-10, D-736): every publish sends `handOver.agents: []`, a list typed in the vendor console reads as drift, and `handoff_agents_body` refuses a Clear-Studio pair, because the vendor keeps a call rather than pass it to an agent on other keys or a pricier model.

**Once per call (D-736).** ThinnestAI never retries an action, but a turn its backup model re-runs can call the same action again (email of 11 Oct 2026, VENDOR-STATED). Every in-call action, our four tools and every client action, runs once per (call, action, meaningful arguments) and a repeat answers with the first answer (`reliability/in_call_once.py`: Redis `SET NX`, 3 h; a repeat arriving mid-run waits up to 6 s, then is told it is in hand; an answer meaning nothing was done is not kept). The per-call WhatsApp and payment-link caps stay a separate cap. The caller lookup answers a repeat `callId` (the phone provider asking again, channels/voice "Who-is-calling lookup") with the first answer and no second lookup, within our 0.8 s; the vendor allows 2 s.

**Trial accounts** (§3c): actions run on a test call; a WhatsApp goes only to the number being called, through the same gates; payment links are refused (`trial_payment_links`).

### 3e. The auto-healer on this engine (D-701)

**Holding a broken agent's line.** The healer's pause is the empty-wallet pause: `override_call_script` sets `answersCalls: false` with the neutral `unavailableMessage` (`api-reference/agents/update-agent.md:940-1017`), stamped on our side as silence reason `healer`, which a top-up does not lift and a publish re-applies. What a caller hears depends on the number's carrier (`channels/voice.md:697-729`).

**Forwarding (D-743, OPERATIONS gate T-26).** A number forward exists: `PATCH /phone-numbers/{number} {forwardTo}` rings the client's phone for every call, before any agent speaks, whether or not the agent answers, and declines only on our plan, balance or busy lines (channels/phone-numbers "Forwarding a whole line", read 11 Oct 2026). The healer holds a broken line by pausing the agent (`answersCalls: false` with the neutral sentence, as before) and forwarding each of its numbers to the client's fallback phone (`campaigns/engine_numbers.forward_agent_numbers`, in each number's own workspace); a refused forward (`forward_destination_not_allowed`, `forward_loop`, `number_cannot_forward`) alarms `healer_forward_refused` and leaves that number on the pause. The restore clears every forward before the publish switches the agent back on (`clear_agent_forwards`; a forward it cannot clear pages `healer_forward_not_cleared`). The agent stays on its numbers, so a forwarded call still carries it and arrives as `call.completed`/`call.analysed` with `forwardedTo` (and in the call list); `ExecutionSnapshot.forwarded` prices our cost at the attached agent's plan (its route rate key; `standard` if it has no route), per connected second in 30-second steps, plus the top-up fee, and at nothing on a number the client brought (`phone_numbers.engine_owned` false), capped at 20 minutes on a rented number, while the client's rung stays the agent's (D-835, `snapshots/2026-10-19/pages/api-reference/calls/get-call.md:1249-1254`; D-813 had priced it at the carrier's fee from the vendor's 18 Oct answer, which its 19 Oct email withdrew). A forward is never recorded (`channels/phone-numbers.md:577-579`): the row carries `calls.forwarded_to_phone`, no recording is fetched for it, the missing-recording watch skips it, and the client's call page says forwarded calls are not recorded. A forwarded call with no agent on the number (none of ours) would appear only in the call list with `agent: null`; we never set one. The agent hand-over forward (`ForwardsLine`) is now only for an engine without a number forward.

**Detection.** The healer reads `calls` as ThinnestAI's `call.completed` and `call.analysed` deliveries write them (§3); time to first audio, knowledge states and caller-language tags are not reported on this engine, so those three signals read zero here. Whether a declined call to a held line still produces a call record is OPERATIONS gate H-4.

**Outage.** `engine_error_spike` counts failed requests to this platform (`engine/health.py`); twice that threshold in ten minutes, broken lines at three clients at once, or the platform's own status reporting calls down opens the platform incident (runbooks/auto-healer.md §3). The status read is `GET https://app.thinnest.ai/api/status`, no key (D-736): named in ThinnestAI's email of 11 Oct 2026 and absent from the docs (searched 11 Oct 2026), so its shape is the one observed that day, `{checkedAt, database, voiceEngine}` with `operational`; any other value is "not operational" and no answer is "unknown". The incident's alarm says whether the platform reports itself operational (look at our side first) or a fault; `database` alone down alarms `engine_platform_status_degraded`. There is no second engine to move to.

**Signals.** A live-call problem (§3 step 4) counts in the agent's health window: `slow_reply` as a slow start, `voice_failed`/`no_answer_from_model`/`own_key_failed` as a failed call.

## 5. Money

ThinnestAI's `costMicro` (millionths of the currency, on `call.analysed` and `GET /calls/{id}`, `snapshots/2026-10-08/pages/api-reference/calls/get-call.md:498-523`) is what a call cost, charged to our one balance whichever workspace the call ran in (`api-reference/customers.md:185-189`; the charges sweep reads each workspace's call log, §3b); it is kept on `calls.engine_charged_inr` and reconciled, never metered (D-690: `engine_call_cost_variance` compares charge × (1 + top-up fee) with the attested minute; a Studio call must carry its Cartesia row). It also catches a call that fell back to the workspace's default keys because one of ours was gone, which is billed for what ran rather than at the route's rate (D-769). It alarms rather than restates. Since 15 Oct 2026 Get Call and `call.analysed` carry each call's `billing` (`snapshots/2026-10-15/pages/api-reference/calls/get-call.md:1228-1294`), parsed into `ExecutionSnapshot.billing` (D-796): on a settled call (`billedSeconds` set) the alarm names the keys and voice plan the call really ran on when they differ from the ones its rate key was sold on (`engine_charges.expected_billing`); names the engine's billed seconds when, on its 30-second step at half the minute rate, they differ from our billed minutes ("Seconds, not rate"); and names its per-minute rate when it differs from the attested one before the fee ("Rate"). **Carrier-only calls (D-813).** A call no agent ran on, one that ended before the agent spoke (`hangup: before_agent`) or one a number forward put through to a phone, is charged the phone carrier's fee only: ₹0.38 a minute in 30-second blocks (₹0.19 each), `billedSeconds` rounded up to the block, and its `voicePlan` and `ratePerMinuteMicro` still name the plan it was set up on, not what was charged, so it is reconciled by `costMicro` (`snapshots/2026-10-18/pages/api-reference/calls/get-call.md:1236-1243`, VENDOR-STATED, and the founder's email of 18 Oct 2026: a 4-second call bills 30 s, ₹0.19). The meter costs it at the operator-attested `carrier` rate (`billing/engine_minutes.CARRIER_RATE_KEY`, a price key and never a route's; migration `d8e3a5c71f42`) on the carrier's 30-second block, so its total matches; its scope and rate are not compared, only its seconds on the block. Until `carrier` is attested such a call meters unpriced and alarms `engine_minute_rate_unattested`, never at zero. This replaces D-743's "standard voice rate" for a forward; `channels/phone-numbers.md:567-570` and `api-reference/phone-numbers/update-phone-number.md:652-654` in the same snapshot still say standard voice rate, while the call's billing schema, the 17 Oct changelog (`whats-new/index.md:28-30`) and the email say carrier fee, and `costMicro` decides it either way. The client's rung stays the agent's. **Free trial calls** (`voicePlan`, `ratePerMinuteMicro` and `billedSeconds` null) are only calls paid from a free-plan workspace's trial minutes (get-call.md:1246-1249): our developer workspace is on a paid plan, so its test calls, our free-trial agents' included, carry plan and rate, and a customer workspace on a trial carries the trial rate. The engine's rate is evidence only; `unit_cost_paid` stays the attested rate (hard rule 7). The hourly sweep reads the call log, which carries no `billing`, so it compares totals; a call it settles itself (metered before the engine settled it) is queued by the write that records its charge and its `billing` is read from List Calls, one listing per workspace holding a queued call, or from Get Call for a row with no `billing` key (before ThinnestAI's release) or a call the listing does not show, at most 20 Get Call reads a run (`engine_charges.check_late_billing`): a throttle, outage or 5xx stops the Get Call reads with nothing spent, another refusal is retried three times, and no database session spans the vendor read. Metering works as follows:
- **Billed minutes:** `ceil(seconds / 30) × 0.5`.
- **Rate:** an operator-attested `THINNEST_INR_PER_MIN`, in NUMERIC INR and console-managed, plus a per-tier rate if we use their voices.
- **Meaning of ₹1/min:** BYOK including telephony. Their own models are ₹1.5 or ₹2.5 (FOUNDER-RELAYED).
- **Unit cost (D-723):** `unit_cost_paid` = billed minutes × attested rate × (1 + wallet top-up fee). The operator attests the posted rate per billed minute, BEFORE the fee (the figure on ThinnestAI's price page and in `costMicro`, which excludes the fee, evaluation §12 item 8); the meter adds the wallet top-up fee, 2.36% on every plan (ThinnestAI email of 12 Oct 2026, D-754; `billing/engine_minutes.topup_fee`), on every rate key, `byok_voice` included. Cartesia's synthesis on a Studio call never enters the wallet and carries no fee. The ops console's per-minute panel shows the all-in figure beside each posted rate. Minutes metered before 11 Oct 2026 without the fee are restated by one compensating `other` row per call (`meta.kind = engine_topup_fee_restatement`), appended by the hourly charge sweep before it compares; client charges do not move.
- **No rate, no sale:** an unattested rate refuses to sell the minute, exactly as the LLM price door does today.
- **Client billing (D-681):** the client is billed in 30-second steps (`rates.client_billed_minutes`), separately from this cost metering.
- **What is sold (D-687, superseding D-681's Premium-only rule):** the vendor `studio` band is sold as **Clear** (₹4.00 on every pack) and the voice-only BYOK leg as **Studio** (the Studio column, ₹7.00 falling to ₹5.50). `standard` and `premium` voices are refused. Publish still refuses an agent with no voice chosen (`engine_voice_required`) and full workspace-keys mode (`engine_own_keys_not_on_sale`). The client rung comes from the stamped `engine_agent_routes.engine_rate_key`, never from `agents.tts_voice`. The Studio leg has its own rate key, `byok_voice` (D-687), for which the operator attests ₹1.50; Cartesia's own charge to our key is a separate cost line through the existing Cartesia pricing.
- **Cost floors (D-687, D-754).** A floor is a margin guard and never the billed cost. ## 6. Compliance (unchanged rules, new enforcement points)

- **Our dial gate still decides every outbound call** (`check_dispatch`). ThinnestAI's own opt-out, do-not-call and 09:00–21:00 checks are a second net, not our control.
- **Person-level writes only in the client's OWN workspace (D-691).** The do-not-call push and the contact erasure below act on a whole ThinnestAI workspace. Both ask `tenancy/engine_workspace.own_workspace` when queued and again when sent, and the client refuses a request without an `org_` workspace header (`engine/thinnest_customer_data.py`), so neither can reach our developer workspace, which holds every legacy client's data. Since D-693 `own_workspace` delegates to the one resolver (§3b), so both act for every tenant whose workspace is `active` and are skipped, as before, for a tenant still waiting for one. A new workspace is filled with the tenant's existing do-not-call list when it activates.
- **Do-not-call is ours, per client, and two-way (founder decision, 8 Oct 2026, revised; D-691).** Our per-client list decides every dial. Each tenant-scoped addition is pushed through the outbox to `POST /do-not-call` in the client's own workspace (`compliance/engine_dnc.py`, job `push_engine_dnc`). A `contact.opted_out` is filed on the list of the client whose agent heard it, through `record_call_optout` (`detected_by: engine_platform`), with the caller's words (`data.reason`, redacted and capped at 160) as its evidence (D-767), so it binds before the next dispatch tick (hard rule 5). A dial ThinnestAI refuses with `do_not_call`/`opted_out` for a number NOT on that client's list is `engine_platform_dnc_block` ("blocked by the voice platform's do-not-call list", `compliance/platform_dnc.py`): it settles the contact like a person-level refusal and adds nothing to the client's list.
- **Erasure and call content (D-691, D-798).** Our copies are always erased by us. ThinnestAI's copy is deleted per call: `DELETE /calls/{id}/content` removes one call's recording (theirs, the carrier's and any training copy), transcript turns, summary and collected fields, keeps the billing record, is idempotent and needs a full key; `409` means live or booked (asked again), `404`/`410` hold nothing to delete, `429`/`503` retry (`snapshots/2026-10-15/pages/api-reference/post-call/delete-call-content.md:443-589`). The outbox job `delete_engine_call_content` (`workers/engine_customer_data.py`) asks it in whichever workspace holds the call, the developer workspace included (a call row is one tenant's), and records `calls.engine_content_deleted_at` / `engine_content_recordings_pending`; it is queued by a DPDP erasure for the subject's calls, by a tenant erasure for every call, and by the nightly retention sweep for calls past the shorter of the tenant's recording and transcript periods, which also re-queues any call still unsettled a day later. Get Call and List Calls' `contentDeleted` is read into `ExecutionSnapshot.content_deleted`, so an emptied call is never fetched for a recording. `DELETE /contacts/{id}` is still workspace-wide (`workspace/data-and-erasure.md:14-56`): only where the tenant has its own workspace does the erasure also queue `erase_engine_contact`, which records each contact as unsettled, then its `recordingsPending`; `contact.erased` (below) sets it to 0. Erasing a contact deletes that person's recordings at once (`workspace/data-and-erasure.md:21`), so while any of the task's calls in that workspace connected, is inside the 90-day floor and has no recording copy of ours, the job records the contacts found as `<id>=held` and sends no DELETE; the nightly retention sweep queues every held erasure again with no subject, and the run that finds the copy held, the floor passed or the call unconnected deletes the recorded contacts (D-810; the rule is D-803's, `compliance/engine_content.calls_awaiting_our_copy`). The `voice_engine` task quotes the raw call ids and is confirmed by code (`processor_erasure.settle_voice_engine_task`) once every call is confirmed with nothing queued and the contact half is settled (`contact-not-asked` in a shared workspace). New proofs say `call_content_and_contact_erasure_requested` or `call_content_deletion_requested`; proofs before D-798 keep their tokens.
- **Call retention (D-798).** `callRetentionDays` on `PATCH /workspace` (with `Thinnest-Workspace` for a client) keeps a workspace's recordings and transcripts 1 to `planRetentionDays` days (30 PAYG, 49 Pro, 75 Scale, 365 Enterprise; above the plan is a 400; null keeps transcripts with no end; `workspace/update-workspace.md:528-599`, `customers.md:243-247`). Every client workspace is set to the shorter of the tenant's recording and transcript policies capped at the plan read from `GET /workspace`, never null (`tenancy/engine_retention.py`): queued at provisioning (`sync_engine_retention`) and read back and repaired hourly with the developer workspace on the new-tenant defaults (`sweep_engine_retention`, alarms `engine_workspace_retention_repaired`/`_drift`). The admin workspace panel shows the days, the plan's window and the last error. No screen lets a tenant change its retention policy today; the hourly check would carry such a change.
- **Workspace events (D-798).** `contact.erased` `{contactId, erasedAt}` and `byok.changed` `{change, kind, provider, byokEnabled, scope}` are subscribed on every agent endpoint and arrive once per endpoint, signed v2 and deduped on the event id; voice-runtime keys them on the event id and refuses one whose `data.workspaceId` names another workspace than the endpoint's (`snapshots/2026-10-15/pages/api-reference/webhooks.md:536-597`). `ingest_engine_workspace_event` maps the workspace to its one tenant (`tenant_for_workspace`, cross-checked with the endpoint's route). `contact.erased` closes the contact half of that tenant's waiting erasure tasks. `byok.changed` runs that workspace's own-key checks at once (Studio's Cartesia key, our Google key; the developer workspace's Studio check), read-first so the echo of our own writes changes nothing, one at a time per workspace under a Redis lease. The hourly sweeps stay: no event is sent for a model change, and saved keys are never re-checked by the platform (`byok_failed` on `call.problem` is the only signal of a key that stopped working).
- **Hard rule 5.**
  - The composed prompt carries the truthful floor and `CONFIDENTIALITY_RULE`.
  - Publish and drift verify both by reading `instructions` back.
  - The 20,000-character cap is checked before publish (`snapshots/2026-10-07/pages/api-reference/agents/update-agent.md:426-431`; the 6 Oct pages said 8,000). The facts stay in knowledge because `instructions` is re-sent, and paid for, on every turn.

The prompt gains one platform-written section, `FACTS_IN_KNOWLEDGE_GUIDANCE`: look the facts up in knowledge, never guess, and say "I don't know" with a callback offer when they are not there. Every hard-rule block (speaking rules, `CONFIDENTIALITY_RULE`, the truthful floor) stays in the prompt and the read-back still verifies them. Every other engine composes byte-for-byte as before (`tests/thinnest_facts_in_knowledge_test.py` pins the digests).
- **D-669, D-708.** The first words verbatim in `greeting` (inbound) and `purpose` (outbound): the notices the agent's switches turn on, then its opening line. The switches never remove the opening line, so an agent with both notices off still greets callers, and an outbound agent is refused (`engine_opening_required`) only when it has no notice switched on and no opening line written.
- **D-674.** The prompt rule only: nothing of ours sits between ThinnestAI's model and its voice (SECURITY-COMPLIANCE §6.1).

  - `recordCalls:true` makes ThinnestAI record.
