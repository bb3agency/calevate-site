# Phone numbers

> Get a number your customers ring, or point one you already own at your agent.

Your agent answers on a phone number. You can get one from us — searched,
bought and ready on the same screen — or use a number you already own.

Numbers belong to the **workspace**, not to one agent. They live on the **Phone
Numbers** page in the main sidebar, which lists every number you hold, where it
came from, and two things for each: the **Inbound** agent, which answers it, and
the **Outbound** agent, which calls out on it. They can be the same agent or
different ones, or either can be empty — see [Inbound and
outbound](#inbound-and-outbound-are-separate). A **Dial-out ready** column says
whether the number can place calls as well as answer them. You can search the
list and move any number to another agent.

<Note>
  You do not need a number to try voice. The website call button works without
  one, on any plan, and it is the same agent with the same voice. A number is
  what lets somebody ring you from a phone — and if you already own one, you can
  point it at your agent on any plan, free included.
</Note>

## Getting a number from us

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Phone Numbers">
    In the main sidebar choose **Phone Numbers**, then **Buy / Import number**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Search">
    Pick a country, and a city if you want a local code. You will see the
    numbers available now, each with what it costs a month.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Take one">
    Choose a number. Before anything is charged you get a summary — the number,
    what it costs a month, what leaves your wallet today and what is left in it —
    and the number is yours the moment you confirm. There is nothing to configure
    with a phone company and no settings to copy anywhere.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Say which agent answers it">
    A new number is not pointed anywhere yet. On the **Phone Numbers** page,
    choose the agent under **Inbound**. That agent starts answering immediately.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Ring it">
    Call the number. You should hear your greeting, then the agent.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### What it costs

Each number has a monthly price, shown before you take it, and it repeats every
month until you give the number up. Your plan may include one — if it does, the
page says so before you choose, and a number your plan includes needs nothing in
your wallet because nothing is taken for it.

**Buying a number needs a paid plan**, because we rent it for you every month.
Pay as you go has no monthly fee of its own and can hold numbers.

**Pointing a number you already own at your agent works on every plan,
including the free one.** It costs us nothing to answer it — you are already
paying your own provider for the number — and a phone call is usually the thing
you want to hear before you decide to pay for anything. Free-plan calls come out
of the same trial minutes as calls from your website.

That covers calls in **both directions**. On the free plan your agent can answer
a number you brought and, once you have added that account's credentials, ring
people from it — your own provider bills you for the line either way. Buying a
number from us is the part that needs a paid plan.

### India needs your business verified first

An Indian number is only issued to a registered business, and the check is in
**your** name rather than ours. It has its own tab — **KYC**, beside Imported on
the Phone Numbers page — because it takes a little time to clear and it is worth
being able to come back and see where it got to.

This is not our rule and we cannot shortcut it. Numbers in most other countries
need nothing beyond choosing one.

<Note>
  This is not the same as [business verification for
  WhatsApp](/meta/verification), which Meta runs and which unlocks a different
  set of things. Passing one does nothing for the other, and you may well need
  both.
</Note>

#### What it asks for

Your registered business name, and **one** document. Which one depends on a
single question:

| Registered for GST? | The document to send |
| - | - |
| Yes | Your **GST certificate** |
| No | Your **Certificate of Incorporation**, or your **Udyam certificate** — whichever you hold |

That is the whole form. PDF, JPG or PNG, up to 5 MB.

<Note>
  Send it as registered. The name you type is checked against the certificate you
  attach, so an abbreviation or a trading name is the usual reason an application
  comes back.
</Note>

#### Following it up

The **KYC** tab shows where the application stands, asked afresh each time you
open the page rather than remembered from last time:

* **Being checked** — sent, usually decided within minutes.
* **Approved** — you can take an Indian number.
* **Not accepted** — the reason is shown with it. **Correct and resend** puts the
  form back up.
* **Suspended** or **Expired** — send your documents again.

Each application has a **reference**. If you ask us to chase one, that is the
thing to quote.

<Note>
  **We keep a copy of the document you sent**, so correcting an application does
  not mean going and finding the file again. You can download it from the same
  tab; that needs an owner or admin.
</Note>

<Warning>
  You cannot buy an Indian number until this is approved — pressing Buy takes you
  to the form instead. Numbers in every other country need none of it.
</Warning>

### Marketing calls need a different number

Indian rules give every number a purpose, and it is worth knowing before you buy
one rather than after you build a campaign.

| What you are calling about | The number for it |
| - | - |
| Reminders, order updates, renewals, anything the customer is expecting | The numbers on this page — landlines, bought in a few clicks |
| **Marketing** — an offer, a promotion, anything selling | A **140** number, which is applied for in your business's name |

A 140 number is not sold from a list. It is issued to your business after you
register on the DLT portal, so choosing **Promotional (140)** when adding a
number shows you the steps rather than a search.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Register your business on the DLT portal">
    As both the *principal entity* and the *telemarketer* — the calls are yours,
    so the registration is yours. Two to three working days.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Send us your certificates">
    Your GST certificate and both DLT registrations. We apply for the number and
    hand you back the approval letter it needs.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Register your header and your script">
    On the same portal. Scripts are approved one at a time — a marketing call
    says an approved thing, not whatever you type on the day.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Five to ten working days end to end. Get in touch and we will start it.

<Warning>
  Check this before anything else: the telemarketer registration has to be in
  **Maharashtra** or **Karnataka**. Registered anywhere else and we cannot get
  you a 140 number today.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Until you hold one, a campaign marked *promotional* is refused rather than
  quietly sent from a landline. That refusal is the rule working — it is the
  number that carries the penalty, not us — and it names the number it is about
  so you know which one to change.
</Note>

## Using a number you already own

If your number is with **Plivo**, **Vobiz**, **Twilio** or **Telnyx**, you can
point it at your agent and keep it where it is. You go on paying them for the
number and we charge you nothing for it — we only answer it. **Twilio and Telnyx
work a little differently** — we set the number up for you; see
[Numbers from Twilio](#numbers-from-twilio) and
[Numbers from Telnyx](#numbers-from-telnyx) below. The steps here are for Plivo
and Vobiz.

<Note>
  **A Plivo, Twilio or Telnyx number needs your account's credentials before it
  can answer.** Plivo and Twilio sign every call with your Auth Token, and we use
  it to confirm each call really came from your account — without it, calls to
  the number are refused. On Telnyx your API key is how we set the number up at
  all. A Vobiz number answers without them; there, the credentials are only
  needed for outgoing calls.
</Note>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Buy / Import number on the Phone Numbers page">
    Choose **Import**, then say which carrier the number is with.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Plivo only: add your account's keys first">
    Paste the **Auth ID** and **Auth Token** from your Plivo console. Plivo signs
    every call with your Auth Token, and that signature is how we tell a real
    call to your number from somebody pretending to be one — so until we have it,
    calls to the number are refused. The same keys are what your outgoing calls
    go out on. (Vobiz needs nothing here to answer calls.)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Type the number">
    Exactly as it is dialled, including the country code.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Point it at us">
    Set the address shown as the number's **Answer URL** in your carrier's
    console, with the method set to **POST**. Copy the whole thing — everything
    after the question mark matters, and a shortened address will not be
    recognised.

    For one of the two carriers the address is the same for every number and is
    shown straight away, so you can set your console up first if you prefer. For
    the other it carries a key unique to the number, so it appears once the
    number is added. Either way that address belongs to the number, so it keeps
    working if you later move the number to a different agent.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Say which agent answers it">
    Back on the **Phone Numbers** page, choose the agent under **Inbound**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **A Plivo number does not answer until its keys are saved.** If you added one
  before the keys were asked for, its row on the **Phone Numbers** page says so —
  open the row's menu, choose **Edit credentials**, and save them.
</Warning>

<Warning>
  Until the Answer URL is set, the number rings and nothing picks up. Nothing on
  this page can tell you that has happened, because the call never reaches us —
  so if a test call goes unanswered, check the Answer URL first.
</Warning>

A number on any other carrier cannot be pointed at us. We check that each call
genuinely came from the carrier it claims to, and a call from anywhere else is
refused before it is answered.

### Numbers from Twilio

A Twilio number is set up the other way round from the two above. Instead of you
pasting an address into your carrier's console, **you give us your Twilio account
and we set the number up for you.**

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose Twilio under Import">
    The credentials box comes first, because Twilio is the one carrier where it
    is needed before the number.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add your Account SID and Auth Token">
    Both are on your Twilio console's home page. The Account SID starts with
    **AC**. We check them with Twilio before saving, and the token is stored
    encrypted and never shown again.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Type the number, with its country code">
    For example **+1 415 555 0100**. A number typed without a country code is
    read as an Indian one, and we will then not find it on your account.
  </Step>

  <Step title="That is it">
    We confirm the number is on your Twilio account and point it at us there.
    The dialog still shows the two addresses we set, in case you ever want to
    check them or set them yourself.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Say which agent answers it">
    On the **Phone Numbers** page, choose the agent under **Inbound**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Pointing a Twilio number at us **replaces whatever it was set to**. If the
  number already runs something of yours — an IVR, a forwarding rule — that stops
  receiving its calls. If it had an address set, the dialog tells you what it was
  so you can put it back, and removing the number here clears the address we set
  and does not restore the old one. Import a number that is free to use.
</Warning>

<Note>
  We need your Auth Token because Twilio signs every call with it, and that
  signature is how we tell a real call to your number from somebody pretending
  to be one. **If you change your Auth Token in Twilio, save the new one** from
  the number's menu (**Edit credentials**) — until you do, calls to the number
  are refused and we tell you so.
</Note>

<Note>
  A workspace works with **one Twilio account**. If you save a different Account
  SID while Twilio numbers from the first account are still added here, we
  refuse it — those numbers would stop receiving calls. Remove the Twilio numbers
  first, then connect the other account. Saving a new Auth Token for the *same*
  account is always fine.
</Note>

<Note>
  If we could not set the number up for you — your Twilio account may have
  restricted it — the dialog says so and shows what to set: in Twilio, open the
  number and set **A call comes in** to the Voice URL and **Call status changes**
  to the status callback, both **HTTP POST**.
</Note>

What a Twilio number does not do yet: **answering-machine detection** (so no
voicemail message) and **transferring a call to a person**. Call recording works
on it — see [Recording calls on a number you brought](#recording-calls-on-a-number-you-brought).

### Numbers from Telnyx

A Telnyx number is set up for you too, and it takes **one API key** rather than
an ID and a token.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose Telnyx under Import">
    The key comes first, for the same reason as on Twilio: we need it before we
    can find the number and set it up.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Paste an API key from your Telnyx portal">
    Create one under **API Keys** in the Telnyx portal. We check it with Telnyx
    before saving, and it is stored encrypted and never shown again. Saving it
    creates an application on your Telnyx account called **ThinnestAI voice
    agent** — that is what answers your number and places your outgoing calls, so
    leave it in place.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Type the number, with its country code">
    For example **+1 415 555 0100**. A number typed without a country code is
    read as an Indian one, and we will then not find it on your account.
  </Step>

  <Step title="That is it">
    We confirm the number is on your Telnyx account and assign it to that
    application. Nothing to paste.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Say which agent answers it">
    On the **Phone Numbers** page, choose the agent under **Inbound**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **Telnyx only calls the countries your account allows.** A new Telnyx account
  allows the United States and Canada. To call India — or anywhere else — add the
  country to your **outbound voice profile** in the Telnyx portal (Voice →
  Outbound Voice Profiles) before you place calls; until then Telnyx refuses
  them, and the call shows as failed here. Telnyx may ask you to verify your
  account before it allows some countries.
</Warning>

<Warning>
  Assigning a Telnyx number to our application **replaces whatever it was
  connected to**. If it was connected to something else of yours, the dialog
  tells you its name so you can move it back; removing the number here takes it
  off our application and does not restore the old one.
</Warning>

<Note>
  A workspace works with **one Telnyx account**. A key from a different account
  is refused while numbers from the first are still added here — remove those
  numbers first. A new key for the *same* account is always fine, and if you had
  deleted our application in Telnyx, saving a key makes a new one and moves your
  numbers onto it.
</Note>

<Note>
  If we could not assign the number for you, the dialog says so. In the Telnyx
  portal, open the number and set its connection to the application called
  **ThinnestAI voice agent**.
</Note>

When we turn a call to a Telnyx number away — no agent set to answer it, or the
workspace out of balance — the caller hears a **busy tone** rather than a
sentence.

A Telnyx number does what a number from us does: it **records**, it **detects
answering machines** (so a campaign can leave a voicemail), and it **transfers a
call to a person**. Telnyx charges your Telnyx account a small fee for each call
it checks for an answering machine, and the call to the person you transfer to
goes on your Telnyx bill like any other call.

### Recording calls on a number you brought

With **Record calls** on, phone calls on a number you brought are recorded too,
whichever company it is with. Your carrier is not asked to record anything — your
agent hears both sides of the call and keeps the recording here, so it plays
with its call in [Usage](/workspace/usage), downloads, and is deleted on your
plan's schedule exactly like any other recording.

### A transferred call on a number you brought

When your agent puts a caller through to a person on a number you brought, **we
stop charging at the moment of the transfer.** From then on the two people talk
on your carrier's line, on your carrier's bill, with no agent involved. (On a
number you took from us the call is billed to its end, because we pay the
carrier for those minutes.)

### Calling out from a number you brought

Outgoing calls go out through the account the number belongs to — and for your
own number, that account is yours, even if your carrier happens to be the same
one we buy numbers from. So there is one more step, and only if you want
outgoing calls: in the same **Import** panel, add the **Auth ID** and **Auth
token** from your carrier's console. (For Plivo, Twilio and Telnyx you have
already done this — it came first.)

We check them against your carrier before saving, so a wrong pair is refused
while you are still looking at the page you copied it from rather than on a call
weeks later. The token is stored encrypted and never shown again.

You can change them later without re-adding the number: the row's menu on the
**Phone Numbers** page has **Edit credentials**. They belong to the carrier
account rather than to one number, so saving applies to every number you brought
from it.

The **Dial-out ready** column on that page says which of your numbers can
already place calls and which are still waiting for their credentials — a number
can answer perfectly while being unable to ring anybody, and that column is where
you would see it.

Once they are saved, calling campaigns and the call API work from that number
exactly as they do from one taken from us.

<Note>
  Answering needs none of this. If the number only ever receives calls, the
  Answer URL above is the whole setup.
</Note>

<Note>
  A number can answer for **one agent**. Typing a number another agent already
  answers is refused rather than quietly moved — that is almost always a
  mistake. Moving one deliberately is a different act: choose the new agent
  under **Inbound** on the **Phone Numbers** page.

  Whenever an agent is about to lose a number — moved to another agent, or set
  back to no agent at all — we ask first and name the agent losing it. Giving a
  number that nobody answers to an agent for the first time takes nothing from
  anybody, so that one just happens.
</Note>

<Note>
  **Getting, moving, renaming, releasing and removing numbers need an owner or
  admin.** Everyone else on the workspace sees the list and what each number is
  doing, which is usually what they came to check.
</Note>

<Note>
  An agent's **Voice** page shows which number that agent answers, and links here
  to change it. Getting a number and choosing who answers it both happen on this
  page, because a number belongs to the workspace rather than to one agent.
</Note>

## What you can do to a number

On the **Phone Numbers** page every row has a menu:

* **Edit credentials** — for a number you brought, the API credentials of the
  carrier account its outgoing calls go through. Saving applies to every number
  you brought from that account.
* **Rename** — a name for your own eyes, so a list of numbers reads as
  "Bengaluru showroom" rather than six strings ending in different digits.
  Callers never see it.
* **Release** — only on a number you rent from us. See below; it is permanent.
* **Remove** — only on a number you brought. We forget it; you keep it. For a
  Twilio number we also clear the address we set on it, and a Telnyx number is
  taken off our application, so it stops ringing at us — but only if it still
  points at us, so anything you have since pointed it at yourself is left alone.

You can also search the list, filter it by where the numbers came from, and
export what you are looking at as a spreadsheet.

## Inbound and outbound are separate

Every number has two pickers on the **Phone Numbers** page, and they are
independent:

| | What it decides |
| - | - |
| **Inbound** | Which agent **answers** when somebody rings the number. One agent, or nobody. |
| **Outbound** | Which agent **calls out** from the number — in campaigns, test calls and the call API. |

They can be the same agent, which is the ordinary case, or different ones.
Some things that is useful for:

* **A receptionist and a caller on one number.** One agent answers; a different
  one makes the outbound calls, and the people it rings see your usual number.
* **A number that only calls out.** Leave **Inbound** empty and choose an agent
  under **Outbound**. Nobody answers when it is rung, but the agent can place
  calls from it.
* **An agent that only calls out.** It does not need a number of its own at all.
  Give it a number under **Outbound** and it can run campaigns and test calls
  straight away — we switch its phone on for you when you do.

**Inbound empty means nobody answers.** Whoever rings the number hears that it is
not taking calls — including somebody ringing back after a campaign call. A
number that is only lent out for calling is never answered by the agent calling
from it. If you want a number to answer, choose an agent under **Inbound**.

When you let a different agent call out on a number that another agent answers,
we ask first, because of exactly that: a customer ringing it back will reach the
agent that **answers** it, not the one that rang them.

### Numbers from different phone companies on one agent

An agent can call out from numbers on **different phone companies** — a
ThinnestAI number and one you imported from Twilio, say — like a phone with two
SIM cards. Each call goes out through **its own number's** company, on its own
account, and hangs up there too.

* **In the Playground**, **Call from** lists every number the agent can call
  from, each with its company's logo. A number that cannot call yet (one you
  imported without saving its credentials) is shown but greyed out.
* **Campaigns** spread their calls across all of them.
* **Some features depend on the company.** Transferring a call to a person and
  leaving a voicemail work on ThinnestAI numbers and numbers from Plivo or
  Telnyx, but not on Vobiz or Twilio numbers. Recording works on every number. A
  call from a number that can't do one of these still goes out — that feature is
  simply off for it, and the Playground and the campaign builder tell you before
  you call.

One limit to know about: **a number you brought needs its credentials saved**
before an agent can call from it, the same as any number of yours that places
calls.

## Spare numbers for calling out

A calling campaign goes out from your agent's number. That is fine for a few
hundred calls and starts to hurt at a few thousand: phone companies and phones
themselves watch for a line that makes a burst of calls, and once a number is
marked as one, **everything it does afterwards suffers** — including a customer
ringing you back and seeing a warning on their screen.

Spare numbers spread that out. Give an agent several numbers under **Outbound**,
and campaigns share their calls across the agent's own number, if it has one, and
the others.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Have a spare">
    Any number on the page — one you bought and have not pointed anywhere, one
    you brought and have not used yet, or one another agent answers.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Lend it">
    Under **Outbound**, choose the agent. That is the whole setup.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**What it does not do.** A spare does not become the agent's number. Nothing
about who answers changes, the number you gave your customers stays the number
they ring, and you can take a spare back at any time by choosing the agent that
answers it, or **Nobody** if none does.

**One person always sees the same number.** Whichever of your numbers rings a
customer is the one that rings them again — including a second attempt days
later. Three attempts from three different numbers is what a nuisance call looks
like, and avoiding that is half the point, so it does not happen.

**Each number has a daily limit.** 200 calls a day, which is also the point:
without one, spreading calls over four numbers is the same burst four times
over. If a campaign uses up every number it says so, and carries on the next
morning — or sooner, if you lend it another.

<Note>
  A spare can be on a different phone company from the agent's other numbers. A
  spare you brought needs its carrier credentials saved, the same as any number of
  yours that places calls. The page says which of these is missing
  rather than letting you find out on a campaign.
</Note>

<Note>
  Changing who answers a number does not change who calls out on it. Moving or
  clearing **Inbound** leaves **Outbound** exactly as it was.
</Note>

<Warning>
  A spare must be licensed for the same kind of calling as the campaign. In
  India promotional calls need a 140-series number, so a campaign marked
  promotional is refused if **any** of the numbers it would use is not one — and
  it names which. It is the number that carries the penalty.
</Warning>

## Disconnecting, giving up, and removing

Three different things and only one of them is permanent.

**Disconnect** takes the number off the agent so it stops answering. The number
stays yours either way — your own number stays with your carrier, and one you
rent from us stays rented and still charged. It shows on the **Phone Numbers**
page with nothing under **Inbound**, ready to point at another agent. An agent
you had chosen under **Outbound** keeps calling out on it.

**Give up** hands a number you rent from us back to the phone company. The
monthly charge stops and the number goes back to the pool.

**Remove** applies only to a number you brought. It takes our record of it away,
so we stop answering it and stop listing it — the line stays yours and your
carrier keeps billing you for it exactly as before. Remember to unset its Answer
URL in your own console afterwards, or calls to it will still reach us and be
declined. Bringing it back later is the same three fields.

<Warning>
  Releasing is permanent. The number goes back to the phone company and somebody
  else can take it — we cannot get it back for you, even a minute later. If it
  is printed anywhere, or anybody has it saved, keep it.
</Warning>

## What callers hear when nothing is set up

* **Answer calls off** — the number does not answer.
* **Answer calls on, Phone number switched off** — the number does not answer. You have
  switched voice on without telling it to pick up the phone.
* **Everything on, no greeting** — the agent waits for the caller to speak
  first. That is a real choice, and some lines are better that way, but it
  surprises people who expected a greeting.

## Things worth knowing

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="One number, one answering agent">
    Two agents cannot both **answer** one number. If you want different behaviour
    for different callers — sales and support, say — that is one agent with
    instructions covering both, or two agents on two numbers.

    Who *calls out* on a number is a separate choice, and an agent can call out
    on several numbers — see **Inbound and outbound are separate** above.
    Answering is still one to one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Trying it on a real phone">
    The **Playground** has a **Try it on a real phone** box under the browser
    call. Type your own number and press **Place call**: the agent rings you from
    its number, so you hear exactly what a customer hears — a phone line, not a
    browser. If the agent can call out on more than one number, a **Call from**
    list lets you choose which, so you can hear a spare as well.

    It is a real call and costs the same per minute as any other. The browser
    call beside it is free and instant, which is the one to use while you are
    editing what the agent says; this one is for hearing how it sounds.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which number do calls out use?">
    When your agent rings somebody, it calls from its own number, or from one you
    have given it under **Outbound**. That is what they see, and what they will
    ring back — and it is answered by whichever agent is under **Inbound** on that
    number, which may not be the one that rang them. See **Inbound and outbound
    are separate** above.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Promotional calling in India needs a different kind of number">
    A normal business number is licensed for service and transactional calls.
    Promotional calling in India needs a 140-series number, and using an
    ordinary one for it can get the number blocked. See [Calling
    campaigns](/channels/voice-campaigns).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Test before you print it">
    Ring your own number and ask the hardest question you get — not "what are
    your hours". The awkward one, with a condition attached, where the honest
    answer is "it depends". That is the answer worth hearing out loud before a
    customer does.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
